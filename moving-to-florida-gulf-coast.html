Which City Is Right for You The Gulf Coast corridor from Tampa to Venice covers about 80 miles and four distinct markets. They share beaches, sunshine, and a lack of state income tax — but the lifestyle, pace, and price point of each are genuinely different. Here's how I match buyers to cities. St. Petersburg Urban · Walkable · Arts & Culture St. Pete has the most urban energy of any Gulf Coast city — a walkable downtown, vibrant arts scene, rooftop bars, independent restaurants, and a 2.5-mile waterfront park. It attracts younger buyers and urban transplants from Chicago and New York who don't want to give up walkability. Entry single-family from ~$450K · Condos from ~$450K · Luxury waterfront $1M–$5M+ Sarasota Sophisticated · Beach Access · Cultural Sarasota offers world-class beaches (Siesta Key is regularly ranked #1 in the U.S.), a refined arts scene anchored by the Ringling Museum and the Sarasota Opera, and a pace slightly more relaxed than St. Pete. Strong draw for buyers from the Northeast who want culture and water. Entry single-family from ~$500K · Luxury $800K–$5M+ · Siesta Key $1M–$10M+ Bradenton / Lakewood Ranch Family · Space · Value Bradenton and Lakewood Ranch offer the best square footage and lot size for the dollar on the Gulf Coast. Lakewood Ranch is one of the top-selling master-planned communities in the U.S., with A-rated schools, walkable town centers, and strong resale demand. Best for families and value-focused buyers. Entry single-family from ~$350K · New construction active · Anna Maria Island $1M+ Tampa Metro · Jobs · South Tampa Premium Tampa is the economic engine of the region — significant employment in financial services, healthcare, tech, and defense. South Tampa (Hyde Park, Davis Islands, Bayshore) commands premium prices and tight inventory. Best for buyers who need proximity to a major corporate employment hub. South Tampa from ~$700K · Suburbs $350K–$600K · Davis Islands $1M–$4M+ Most buyers I work with narrow to one or two cities fairly quickly once we talk through lifestyle, commute needs, budget, and whether they have kids in school. If you're genuinely torn, I typically recommend a 3-day trip to walk each neighborhood in person — what you feel on foot usually resolves it faster than any amount of research online. Florida Tax Advantages The tax picture is the most concrete financial case for relocating to Florida, and it's significant enough to be a primary driver for many buyers — not just a bonus. Tax Type Florida Illinois New York California State income tax None 4.95% flat 4–10.9% 1–13.3% Estate / inheritance tax None None (estate) 3.06–16% None Property tax rate ~0.8–1.2% effective ~2.0–2.5% effective ~1.4–1.9% ~0.7–1.1% Homestead exemption Up to $50,000 off assessed value Up to $10,000 Varies Prop 13 limits increases For a household earning $300,000 annually relocating from Illinois, the state income tax savings alone — $14,850 per year at Illinois's 4.95% rate — offsets a meaningful portion of higher insurance costs. For high earners moving from New York or California, the savings are even more dramatic. Florida's Homestead Exemption also caps the annual increase in assessed value at 3% per year (or the rate of inflation, whichever is lower) once you establish primary residence. This is called the Save Our Homes cap and it protects long-term owners from property tax increases even as market values rise. Important To claim Florida residency for tax purposes, you typically need to establish Florida as your primary domicile — driver's license, voter registration, and a signed Declaration of Domicile filed with the county clerk. I can refer you to a Florida tax attorney for specifics, but the process is straightforward for most buyers. The Insurance Reality Florida's insurance market is the thing that most surprises out-of-state buyers — and the thing I spend the most time explaining. If you're moving from Chicago or New York, you are almost certainly going to pay significantly more for homeowners insurance in Florida than you did in your previous home. It's not a small difference. The primary drivers are wind exposure (Florida is in a hurricane zone) and flood risk (much of coastal Florida is in FEMA-designated flood zones). Insurance companies price both risks into their policies, and after several active hurricane seasons, available carriers have contracted and premiums have risen substantially. Call a Florida independent insurance broker with the property address and get a real number. I refer every relocation client to brokers I trust for exactly this reason. Wind mitigation inspections, elevation certificates, and choosing newer construction (built to post-2004 wind codes) can all meaningfully reduce your premiums. These are not minor differences — a wind mitigation inspection on the right property can reduce the wind portion of your premium by 30–50%. Moving from Chicago Chicago is my single largest source of relocation buyers. There's an established and growing community of Chicago transplants across St. Pete, Sarasota, and Bradenton — you will not be the first person from the North Shore or Lincoln Park to land on the Gulf Coast, and that community is genuinely welcoming. The financial case from Illinois is particularly compelling. Illinois's 4.95% flat income tax means a household earning $250,000 is paying approximately $12,375 per year to the state — money that evaporates in Florida. Combined with lower property tax rates on comparable homes (Illinois property taxes are among the highest in the nation), most Chicago buyers see meaningful improvement in their overall tax picture. Chicago O'Hare and Midway both offer direct flights to Tampa International (2 hours 15 minutes) and Sarasota-Bradenton International (2 hours 30 minutes). Southwest, United, American, and Spirit all serve these routes. Keeping ties to family, friends, and business in Chicago is straightforward — most of my Chicago clients fly back 4–6 times per year and find it more manageable than they expected. Best Gulf Coast matches for Chicago buyers: Old Northeast and Kenwood in St. Pete appeal strongly to buyers from Lincoln Park, Wicker Park, and Andersonville who want walkability and neighborhood character. Lakewood Ranch draws buyers from the North Shore and western suburbs who want space, good schools, and a family-oriented community. Sarasota's downtown and bayfront appeal to buyers from the Gold Coast who want culture and sophistication. Moving from New York New York buyers come to the Gulf Coast for different reasons than Midwesterners — the tax picture is even more dramatic (New York City residents can face combined state and city income tax above 14%), but the lifestyle shift is also more pronounced. Pace of life, cost of a comparable home, and the availability of outdoor space are all significant factors. New York metro buyers tend to gravitate toward Sarasota more than St. Pete — the cultural infrastructure (opera, ballet, theater, world-class museums), the bayfront setting, and the proximity to Siesta Key resonate with buyers from the Upper East Side, Connecticut, and Westchester. St. Pete's urban energy appeals to buyers from Brooklyn and downtown Manhattan who want walkability. The price comparison is often striking. A $2.5M budget in Manhattan buys a 2-bedroom condo. The same budget on the Gulf Coast buys a waterfront estate on Siesta Key or Snell Isle. That delta is one of the most consistent things I hear from New York buyers — the shock of what their money actually buys here. JetBlue, Delta, American, and United all operate direct and near-direct service from JFK, LGA, and EWR to Tampa and Sarasota. The flight is approximately 3 hours. Common Questions What are the tax advantages of moving to Florida from Illinois or New York? Florida has no state income tax. For a household earning $300,000 annually, that's approximately $14,850 in annual savings versus Illinois (4.95% flat) or $20,000–$30,000+ versus New York (state + city). Florida also has no estate or inheritance tax. The Homestead Exemption reduces your property's assessed value by up to $50,000 and caps annual assessment increases at 3% or inflation, whichever is lower. Can I buy a home in Florida without being there in person? You can, but I don't recommend it. Most relocation buyers can complete a successful purchase with one 2–3 day trip to Florida. I handle the virtual prep — neighborhood research, video walkthroughs, property shortlisting — so your time in Florida is spent seeing the right things. Offer, contract, due diligence, and closing can all be handled remotely via DocuSign and mail-away closing. How much does homeowners insurance cost in Florida vs. Illinois or New York? Expect to pay significantly more. A comparable home that cost $2,000/year to insure in the Chicago suburbs might cost $6,000–$12,000 in a Gulf Coast non-flood-zone location, or $15,000–$25,000+ in a waterfront flood zone. The income tax savings typically offset this for most buyers, but you need to know the insurance number before you make an offer — not after. What's the best Gulf Coast city for someone moving from Chicago? It depends on what you're leaving behind and what you're looking for. Buyers from Chicago's walkable neighborhoods (Lincoln Park, Wicker Park, Old Town) tend to love Old Northeast and Kenwood in St. Pete. Buyers from the North Shore or suburbs who want space and schools tend to land in Lakewood Ranch or Bradenton. Buyers who want the most cultural amenities in a smaller-city setting tend to choose Sarasota. I've placed Chicago buyers in all of them — the right fit depends on your specific priorities.
