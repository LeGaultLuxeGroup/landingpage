CDD Fees in Sarasota County: What Lakewood Ranch, Wellen Park, and North Port Buyers Really Pay (2026)

How much are CDD fees in Sarasota County, and how do they affect what you pay?

Most Sarasota County homes inside a Community Development District (CDD) or similar special district pay roughly $1,000–$3,000 per year, and some newer Lakewood Ranch phases have topped $6,000 a year in combined district fees. The charges appear as non-ad valorem assessments on your Sarasota County property tax bill, are not reduced by the homestead exemption, and usually have two parts: a bond (debt service) payment that ends after the bonds are repaid and an operations and maintenance (O&M) charge that continues. Before you make an offer, look up the parcel's exact CDD line items and remaining bond balance, then factor them into your monthly payment.

What a CDD Actually Is (and Why It Isn't Your HOA)

A Community Development District is a special-purpose local government created under Chapter 190 of the Florida Statutes. Developers use CDDs to finance roads, water and sewer lines, stormwater ponds, landscaping, and sometimes amenity centers through tax-exempt bonds. Then the homeowners repay those bonds over time, typically over 20–30 years.

Some of the largest communities here use a different kind of district that works the same way for you as a buyer:

  • Lakewood Ranch uses the Lakewood Ranch Stewardship District, created by a special act of the Legislature, along with a handful of Chapter 190 CDDs. The Sarasota County Tax Collector's levying list includes Sarasota-side districts such as LT Ranch CDD, Central Parc CDD, Lakes of Sarasota CDD, and Rustic Oaks CDD.
  • Wellen Park sits inside the West Villages Improvement District (WVID), which covers about 12,444 acres in the City of North Port and unincorporated Sarasota County. Assessments are set separately for each "unit" of the district, so two Wellen Park neighborhoods a mile apart can carry very different numbers.
  • Venice, Nokomis, and North Port have additional CDDs tied to individual master-planned communities. Check every new-construction community you're considering.

Here's how a CDD differs from an HOA:

  • A CDD is a government. Its board holds public meetings, publishes its budget, and bills you through the county tax roll. If you don't pay, the result is a tax certificate on the property, not a letter from a management company.
  • An HOA is a private association that enforces deed restrictions, runs the gatehouse, and bills you monthly or quarterly.
  • Many communities have both. One recent Wellen Park listing showed a district assessment of $4,095.95 per year plus an HOA of $366.79 per month. That's more than $8,400 a year before property taxes and insurance.

The two parts of every CDD bill

  • Debt service (bond) assessment. This repays the infrastructure bonds tied to your lot. It's fixed, and it ends when the bonds are paid off.
  • O&M assessment. This pays to maintain the district's ponds, common areas, and landscaping. It's set each year in the district budget and doesn't end. For example, the Lakewood Ranch Stewardship District's proposed FY2026 O&M assessment was capped at $658.32 per residential unit at build-out.

Why does the split matter? On a resale, a home built 15 years ago may have only a few years of bond payments left, or the seller may have paid them off already. A brand-new home in the same area could be at the start of a 30-year bond.

What Sarasota County Buyers Actually Pay

A 2025 Suncoast Searchlight analysis reported by WUSF found that more than two-thirds of analyzed district properties in the region paid $1,000–$3,000 per year in district fees. Homeowners in some Lakewood Ranch phases paid more than $6,300, and one CDD had a median of nearly $4,000.

Here's what that does to your monthly payment and buying power:

  • $1,500/year CDD = about $125/month added to your escrow
  • $3,000/year CDD = about $250/month. At a 6.5% 30-year rate, that payment could have covered about $40,000 more mortgage.
  • $4,100/year CDD (the Wellen Park example) = about $340/month

Your lender counts the CDD in your debt-to-income ratio because it's collected with your taxes. A home that fits your budget on paper can push you out of qualifying once the full escrow is added.

Also keep in mind that a CDD isn't affected by the tax changes that apply to your home's assessed value. The Sarasota County homestead exemption, the Save Our Homes cap, and the Amendment 3 property tax changes reduce ad valorem taxes, which are based on value. CDD assessments are non-ad valorem, so they stay the same no matter what your home is worth or which exemptions you qualify for.

What about neighborhoods without a CDD?

Established Sarasota neighborhoods such as Southside Village, Gulf Gate, Sarasota Springs, and much of Downtown Sarasota were built before CDDs were common, so most homes there have none. Outside Wellen Park, many North Port lots also have no CDD, but the City of North Port bills its own non-ad valorem assessments (road and drainage, fire rescue, and solid waste). Every property's tax bill tells the real story, so I never assume.

How to Check a Property's CDD Before You Make an Offer

This is the routine I walk my buyers through on every new-construction or master-planned community home:

  1. Pull the parcel on the Sarasota County Property Appraiser site (sc-pa.com). Note the parcel ID and whether it's in the City of Sarasota, the City of North Port, the City of Venice, or unincorporated county.
  2. Open the current tax bill on the Sarasota County Tax Collector site (sarasotataxcollector.gov). Under non-ad valorem assessments, you'll see each district by name and amount, such as WVID or LWR Stewardship District.
  3. Separate debt service from O&M. The tax bill may combine them. The district's adopted budget and assessment methodology, both posted on the district website, break them out by unit or product type.
  4. Ask for the remaining bond balance and payoff amount. The district manager can tell you what's left on the lot and whether the debt portion can be prepaid. Prepaying ends the bond payments, but O&M continues.
  5. For new construction, read the disclosure. Florida Statute § 190.048 requires the builder's contract for the initial sale of a lot in a CDD to include a bold disclosure right above your signature line. Read the builder's estimated assessment and confirm whether it's at full build-out or a reduced early-phase number.
  6. Plug the real number into your lender's estimate. Don't let anyone quote you a payment that leaves it out.

Negotiating the CDD into your deal

Here's where local experience pays off. Sarasota County's 2026 market favors buyers in many new-construction communities, and builders in Wellen Park, Lakewood Ranch, and North Port are offering rate buydowns and closing-cost credits to move inventory. You can direct those incentives at the CDD:

  • Ask the builder to prepay part or all of the bond balance instead of offering a design-center credit. That's a permanent cut to your monthly payment, while a design credit only covers upgrades.
  • On a resale, price the bond balance into your offer. In a standard Florida resale contract, the current year's assessment is usually prorated at closing and you take over the remaining bond balance. A home with $20,000 left on its bond isn't the same deal as an identical home that's paid off.
  • Compare the total monthly cost, not the price. A $625,000 home with no CDD can cost less each month than a $595,000 home with a $3,500 CDD.

Builder incentives change monthly and vary by community. Before you sign anything, get the real CDD number and run the comparison with someone who sees these contracts every week. I also cover builder contracts in more depth in my Sarasota pre-construction buyer's guide.

Frequently Asked Questions

Are CDD fees included in my Sarasota County property tax bill?

Yes. CDD and special district assessments appear as non-ad valorem assessments on your annual Sarasota County tax bill, which goes out around November 1. If you have a mortgage, they're usually paid through your escrow account along with your property taxes.

Does the homestead exemption reduce CDD fees?

No. The homestead exemption and the Save Our Homes cap reduce only ad valorem taxes, which are based on your home's assessed value. CDD assessments are non-ad valorem, so a homesteaded home and a second home in the same CDD unit pay the same assessment.

Do CDD fees ever go away?

The debt service portion ends when the district's bonds are paid off, typically 20–30 years after issuance, or sooner if the bond balance on your lot is prepaid. The operations and maintenance portion continues for as long as the district exists and can rise or fall with its annual budget.

Can I pay off my CDD bond early in Lakewood Ranch or Wellen Park?

Many districts allow owners to prepay the debt service balance on their lot. Ask the district manager for a written payoff amount. Prepaying removes the bond line from future tax bills, but you'll still pay the annual O&M assessment.

Is a CDD the same as an HOA?

No. A CDD is a unit of local government that finances and maintains public infrastructure and bills you through the county tax roll. An HOA is a private association that enforces deed restrictions and bills you directly. Many Sarasota County master-planned communities have both, so budget for both.

CDD fees aren't a reason to skip Lakewood Ranch, Wellen Park, or North Port. These communities offer newer construction, amenities, and infrastructure you won't find in older neighborhoods. But the CDD is a real, recurring part of what you'll pay, and in some cases it's large enough to change which home is the better deal.
