Florida Property Tax Reform 2026 (Amendment 3): What Sarasota County Buyers and Sellers Need to Know

Florida voters approved Amendment 3 on November 3rd — expanding homestead exemption protections. Here's exactly what it means for your Sarasota County transaction, and the deadline you can't miss.

Quick Answer

What does Florida Amendment 3 (HJR 1F) mean for Sarasota County home buyers and sellers?

Florida voters approved Amendment 3 on November 3, 2026, expanding homestead exemption protections for Florida primary residence owners. For Sarasota County buyers, the reform reduces annual property tax bills — but only if you apply by March 1, 2027. For sellers, expanded buyer protections increase purchasing confidence and may strengthen near-term demand. The changes take effect for the 2027 tax year. Here's what both sides of a Sarasota County transaction need to know and do right now.

Florida's big property tax vote came and went on November 3rd. If you're buying or selling a home in Sarasota County right now, it matters — and most people haven't fully caught up with what it means for them.

Amendment 3, the constitutional amendment tied to HJR 1F ("Save Our Homes from Excessive Property Taxes"), passed with strong voter support across Florida. It expands existing homestead exemption protections, building on the state's already-favorable tax treatment for primary residence owners. The changes apply to the 2027 tax year — which means buyers closing in the next few months have a narrow window to capture the full benefit from day one.

Here's what you need to know, by the numbers, before your next move.

How property taxes actually work in Sarasota County

Before we get to what changed, it helps to understand how Florida property taxes are calculated — because it's not what most out-of-state buyers expect when they walk through a home on Lido Key or close on a Palmer Ranch new build.

Florida taxes your property based on assessed value, not market value. And assessed value can be significantly lower than what you paid, once two key protections are in play.

First: the homestead exemption. If this is your primary residence, a portion of your home's assessed value is exempt from taxation. That reduction comes directly off your taxable value — which directly reduces your bill.

Under Florida's pre-reform framework, the homestead exemption reduced taxable value by up to $50,000 — the first $25,000 fully exempt from all taxes, the next $25,000 exempt from non-school millage only. Amendment 3 raised the homestead exemption to $150,000 — a dramatic expansion that meaningfully lowers annual tax bills for primary residence owners at virtually every Sarasota County price point. For a home assessed at $750,000, that's $100,000 more of your assessed value shielded from taxation compared to the pre-reform exemption.

Second: the Save Our Homes cap. Once you establish homestead, the county can only increase your assessed value by 3% per year (or the rate of inflation — whichever is lower). That's the protection that makes long-term Sarasota homeowners' tax bills look dramatically lower than their neighbors who just bought.

Sarasota County's total millage rate varies by location. Areas inside the City of Sarasota, Venice, or North Port pay different amounts than unincorporated Sarasota County — and special districts (mosquito control, fire, drainage) layer on additional fractions. That's why a home in Gulf Gate can carry a meaningfully different tax rate than a comparable home in Palmer Ranch or the Nokomis corridor, even at the same price point.

What Amendment 3 changed — and why it matters right now

Amendment 3 expands the protections available to Florida homeowners, with the most direct near-term impact on buyers establishing homestead for the first time.

The reform strengthens the homestead exemption framework, increasing the value of the tax relief available to qualified primary residence owners. For buyers purchasing in Sarasota County's $500K–$1.5M price bands — the sweet spot of this market — the expanded protections translate to lower annual carrying costs compared to what the pre-reform exemption delivered.

The timing matters enormously. Amendment 3 passed just 8 days ago. The search for information about what it means — for buyers, for sellers, for investors considering a Florida property — is at its absolute peak right now. And the window to act and capture the benefit from day one is closing as we move toward the 2027 application deadline.

For buyers considering Sarasota County versus other Florida markets like Tampa or Naples: the benefit is similar statewide, but Sarasota County's specific millage structure means the absolute dollar savings will reflect your exact location within the county. The full cost picture for Sarasota County buyers includes not just property taxes but insurance — and both are worth understanding before you make an offer.

Critical Deadline

March 1, 2027 — the homestead exemption application deadline for the 2027 tax year. Miss this date and you'll pay a full year's taxes without the exemption benefit. File before it and the reduced taxable value applies to the entire 2027 tax year.

What this means if you're buying in Sarasota County right now

If you're under contract, in due diligence, or planning to close in the next few months, the most important date on your calendar is now March 1, 2027.

Here's how to make sure you capture the full benefit from day one:

  • Apply through the Sarasota County Property Appraiser's office at scpafl.org. The application is straightforward, but you need to have the right documents ready: your recorded deed, a Florida driver's license or state ID showing the property address as your primary residence, and your Social Security number.
  • Establish residency by January 1, 2027. You need to be living in the home as your primary residence on January 1st of the application year. If you close in December 2026, that means being moved in and established before New Year's Day.
  • Ask about portability if you're relocating from another Florida home — more on this below.
  • Understand your assessed value vs. your purchase price. The county will likely assess your property near your sale price in year one, but once homestead is in place, the Save Our Homes cap kicks in and limits future assessment increases to 3% per year.

The property tax savings compound significantly over time. As Sarasota County market values rise — and the trajectory in desirable areas like Downtown Sarasota, Bird Key, and the Palmer Ranch corridor has been consistently upward — your assessed value is capped at that 3% annual increase. Neighbors who buy later start from a higher base. That gap grows every year you hold the property.

What this means if you're selling in Sarasota County

For sellers, Amendment 3 is indirectly good news — but it also reveals a nuance worth understanding before you price your home.

Lower long-term carrying costs make buyers more confident in their purchase. If a buyer in your price range knows they can lock in strong homestead exemption benefits under Amendment 3 — and cap future assessment increases under Save Our Homes — that's one more reason to act now rather than wait.

The more direct issue for sellers is understanding the gap between your tax bill and your buyer's projected tax bill. If you've owned your Sarasota home for several years with homestead in place, your assessed value is probably well below your current market value. When you sell, that assessment resets for the buyer — they'll be taxed based on the sale price (or the assessed value at next reassessment), not what you've been paying.

This comes up constantly when sellers are surprised by their net proceeds. The number you've been paying in property taxes for years isn't the number your buyer's lender is using for their PITI calculation. If your taxes were low because of a low assessed value and a Save Our Homes cap built up over a decade of ownership, your buyer's carrying costs will be significantly higher than yours — and that's worth factoring into your pricing conversation and your buyer's offer evaluation.

Your closing costs as a seller are unaffected by Amendment 3 — you'll still pay documentary stamp taxes ($0.70 per $100 of sale price) and the full title insurance breakdown specific to Sarasota County. The tax reform is strictly about what happens after the sale, once the buyer establishes ownership and applies for homestead.

Portability: the move-up buyer advantage most people miss

If you're currently a Florida homeowner and you're selling to buy in Sarasota County, don't skip this part.

Florida law allows you to transfer your accumulated Save Our Homes benefit — the gap between your assessed value and your market value — to a new primary residence. This is called portability, and it can be significant.

Here's how it works in practice: if you've owned a home in Tampa with homestead for 8 years, your assessed value might be $200,000 below your market value thanks to the Save Our Homes cap. When you sell and buy in Sarasota County, you can apply to bring up to $500,000 of that accumulated benefit to your new property — essentially giving you a head start on the Save Our Homes protection from day one, rather than having your assessed value reset to your purchase price.

You file for portability through the Sarasota County Property Appraiser's office at the same time you apply for your new homestead exemption. The deadline is the same: March 1st of the year after your purchase. The application asks for documentation from your previous county's property appraiser showing your prior homestead status and assessed value.

Some move-up buyers closing on a Sarasota home without working with a local agent miss this entirely. If you've had homestead in place for more than 2–3 years in another Florida county, it's worth getting a portability estimate from the Sarasota County Property Appraiser's office before you close — the numbers can be meaningful enough to factor into your decision.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I apply for the homestead exemption in Sarasota County after closing?

You apply through the Sarasota County Property Appraiser's office at scpafl.org. You'll need your recorded deed, a Florida driver's license or state ID showing the property address as your primary residence, and your Social Security number. The deadline is March 1st of the year following your purchase. You must have been residing in the home as your primary residence on January 1st of that year — so if you close in late December, moving in and establishing residency before January 1st is essential.

How much will the homestead exemption save me on my Sarasota County property taxes?

Your savings depend on your home's assessed value and the total millage rate where you're buying — which varies by municipality and special districts across Sarasota County. On a $700,000 home in unincorporated Sarasota County (roughly 17–19 total mills), the homestead exemption can reduce your annual tax bill by $900–$1,400 per year at current exemption levels, with Amendment 3's expanded protections increasing that benefit further for the 2027 tax year. The Sarasota County Property Appraiser's office can give you a property-specific estimate before you close.

What is the Save Our Homes cap and how does it work in Sarasota County?

Save Our Homes limits annual increases to your property's assessed value to 3% or the rate of inflation — whichever is lower — once you've established homestead exemption. This means your tax bill is protected from rising as fast as market values over time. After several years of homestead in a rising market like Sarasota County, your assessed value may be significantly below your market value, keeping your property taxes far lower than neighbors who purchased more recently — a compounding benefit that grows every year you hold the property.

What does Florida Amendment 3 mean for sellers in Sarasota County?

For sellers, Amendment 3 strengthens buyer confidence by lowering the long-term carrying costs for buyers who establish homestead — which may support demand in Sarasota County, particularly in the $400K–$1.5M range where property tax costs meaningfully affect affordability calculations. The more direct impact for sellers is understanding that your buyer's tax picture will reset to market value at purchase, meaning their projected taxes will likely be higher than yours — worth factoring into your list price strategy and your response to offers.

Can I transfer my existing homestead exemption if I'm moving from another Florida home to Sarasota County?

Yes — Florida's portability provision allows you to transfer your accumulated Save Our Homes benefit to a new primary residence, up to $500,000 of accumulated benefit. You apply for portability at the same time as your new homestead exemption, through the Sarasota County Property Appraiser's office, with a March 1st deadline the year after your purchase. If you've had homestead in place in another Florida county for several years, the portability benefit can be substantial — it's worth requesting a portability estimate before you finalize your purchase decision.
