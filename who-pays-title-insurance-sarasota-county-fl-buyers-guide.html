Who Pays Title Insurance in Sarasota County, FL? A Buyer's Guide to Closing Costs (2026)

Who pays title insurance in Sarasota County, Florida?

In Sarasota County, the buyer pays for the owner's title insurance policy — not the seller. This is the opposite of the custom in Pinellas County (St. Petersburg, Clearwater, Dunedin), where the seller typically covers that cost. Florida title insurance premiums are state-regulated, so the rate itself is identical regardless of who pays; on a $700,000 purchase, the owner's policy runs approximately $3,575, and on a $1.2 million purchase, approximately $5,575. Understanding this convention before you make an offer is the difference between a closing that goes smoothly and one that produces sticker shock.

If you've closed on a home anywhere else in the Tampa Bay area and you're now looking in Sarasota, you're going to notice a difference in your closing cost estimate — and it's a big one.

In Pinellas County, it's standard practice for the seller to pay for the owner's title insurance policy. In Sarasota County, that cost lands on the buyer. Same state, same policy, different county — different custom. This is one of the most common surprises I see when clients come to me having previously purchased in St. Pete or Clearwater, and it's absolutely something you need to account for before you make an offer.

Here's exactly how it works.

What Is Title Insurance and Why Does It Matter?

Title insurance protects you from problems with the property's ownership history. Think unknown liens, unpaid contractor bills, fraudulent deed transfers, clerical errors in county records, or disputes from a prior owner's heir. If any of those surface after you close, your title insurance policy defends your ownership and covers any resulting losses.

In Florida, there are two policies involved in most transactions:

  • Owner's title insurance policy — protects you, the buyer, for as long as you own the home (and beyond, in many cases)
  • Lender's title insurance policy — protects your mortgage lender; required if you're financing the purchase

The buyer almost always pays for the lender's policy, in every Florida county. What varies by county is who pays for the owner's policy — and in Sarasota, that's you.

What Does Title Insurance Actually Cost in Sarasota County?

Florida is one of a handful of states where title insurance premiums are promulgated — meaning the state sets the rates. Every title company in Sarasota County charges the same premium for the same coverage. You're not shopping for a better price; you're shopping for service quality and closing experience.

The rate schedule works like this:

  • First $100,000 of purchase price: $5.75 per $1,000
  • $100,001 to $1,000,000: $5.00 per $1,000
  • $1,000,001 to $5,000,000: $2.50 per $1,000

Here's what that looks like in real numbers at Sarasota's typical price points:

On a $700,000 Purchase

  • First $100K: $575
  • Remaining $600K at $5.00/thousand: $3,000
  • Owner's title insurance: ~$3,575 (buyer pays in Sarasota)
  • Lender's policy (simultaneous issue, ~80% LTV): ~$200–$250
  • Doc stamps to seller: $700,000 × 0.70% = $4,900
  • Intangible tax on $560K mortgage: $1,120

On a $1,200,000 Purchase

  • First $100K: $575
  • Next $900K at $5.00/thousand: $4,500
  • Remaining $200K at $2.50/thousand: $500
  • Owner's title insurance: ~$5,575 (buyer pays in Sarasota)
  • Lender's policy (simultaneous issue, ~80% LTV): ~$300–$350
  • Doc stamps to seller: $1,200,000 × 0.70% = $8,400
  • Intangible tax on $960K mortgage: $1,920

If you're paying cash, you skip the lender's policy and the intangible tax — but the owner's title insurance still applies, and in Sarasota County, it's still your cost to bear.

Your Full Buyer Closing Cost Picture in Sarasota County

Title insurance is the biggest variable, but it's not the only line item. Here's the full picture of what most buyers pay at closing in Sarasota:

Typically buyer's responsibility:

  • Owner's title insurance premium (state-promulgated rate)
  • Lender's title insurance premium (simultaneous issue, if financing)
  • Title company/closing fee: $500–$1,000 depending on the firm
  • Intangible tax on the mortgage: 0.2% of loan amount
  • Doc stamps on the mortgage note: $0.35 per $100 of loan amount
  • Recording fees: typically $18–$30 per document
  • Lender origination and processing fees (varies by lender)
  • Appraisal: $500–$800 for most Sarasota-area price points
  • Home inspection: $400–$700 depending on home size
  • Prepaid homeowners insurance (typically 1 year upfront)
  • Prepaid property taxes (varies by closing date)
  • Prepaid interest (from closing date to end of month)
  • HOA transfer fee and/or capital contribution if applicable

In total, budget 2%–4% of the purchase price in buyer closing costs in Sarasota County. On a $700,000 home, that's roughly $14,000–$28,000 depending on your loan type and lender fees. On a $1.2 million home, it's $24,000–$48,000.

These ranges can feel wide, but the variation usually comes down to lender fees, HOA costs, and how many days of prepaid interest you owe at closing. The only way to get precise numbers is to get a Loan Estimate from your lender and request a Closing Disclosure review with your agent.

A Few Sarasota-Specific Things Worth Knowing

If you're buying in downtown Sarasota, the Rosemary District, or Burns Court, condo purchases have an added layer: HOA and condo association transfer fees that vary by building. In some of the newer downtown developments, capital contribution fees (sometimes called "buy-in" fees) can add $2,000–$10,000 or more to your closing costs. Always request the condo documents and HOA fee schedule before making an offer.

For Lakewood Ranch buyers — this is a master-planned community with multiple sub-associations. Most communities require both a transfer fee and a capital contribution to the master CDD (Community Development District) at closing. Your agent should pull the CDD disclosure and confirm which fees apply to the specific home you're considering.

For waterfront purchases on Bird Key, Lido Key, or Osprey, if the property is in a FEMA flood zone, your lender will require flood insurance at or before closing. That first-year premium needs to be paid upfront and factored into your cash-to-close. Florida flood insurance rates — whether NFIP or private — have shifted substantially, so confirm current quotes before you're under contract.

Your specific closing cost number depends on your home's location, loan type, which associations apply, and the contract terms you negotiate. That's where having a local agent who knows how these numbers actually stack up in Sarasota makes a real difference.

Frequently Asked Questions

Who pays title insurance in Sarasota County, Florida?

In Sarasota County, it is customary for the buyer to pay for the owner's title insurance policy. This is the opposite of Pinellas County (St. Petersburg, Clearwater), where the seller typically covers that cost. Florida title insurance rates are set by the state, so the premium itself is the same regardless of who pays — the difference is simply which party writes the check.

How much does title insurance cost in Sarasota County?

Florida title insurance premiums are promulgated (state-regulated), calculated at approximately $5.75 per $1,000 on the first $100,000 of purchase price, then $5.00 per $1,000 up to $1 million, then $2.50 per $1,000 above $1 million. On a $700,000 home, the owner's policy runs approximately $3,575. On a $1.2 million home, expect approximately $5,575. A simultaneous-issue lender's policy (issued at closing alongside the owner's policy) is typically issued at a significant discount.

Who pays doc stamps in Sarasota County?

In Florida, including Sarasota County, the seller pays the documentary stamp tax (doc stamps) on the deed at a rate of $0.70 per $100 of the sale price. On a $700,000 sale, that's $4,900 out of the seller's proceeds. The buyer pays the intangible tax on their mortgage at a rate of $0.002 per dollar of loan amount, plus doc stamps on the mortgage note at $0.35 per $100 of the loan.

How much are total closing costs for a buyer in Sarasota County?

Total buyer closing costs in Sarasota County typically run 2%–4% of the purchase price, depending on loan type, lender fees, and HOA factors. On a $700,000 home with a conventional loan, budget approximately $14,000–$28,000 in closing costs, which includes the owner's title insurance premium (~$3,575), lender's title insurance, intangible tax, recording fees, prepaid property taxes and insurance, and lender origination fees.

Is it negotiable who pays title insurance in Sarasota County?

Yes — in Florida, who pays for title insurance is a negotiable term in the purchase contract, not a law. The local customs (buyer pays in Sarasota, seller pays in Pinellas) are just conventions. In a competitive offer situation, a buyer could offer to pay title insurance even in Pinellas County to strengthen their bid. In Sarasota, a seller might agree to cover it as a concession. Everything is negotiable — which is why reviewing the purchase contract carefully with your agent matters before you sign anything.
