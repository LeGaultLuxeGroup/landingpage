Homeowners Insurance in Sarasota, FL: What to Know Before You Close (2026)

Citizens Property Insurance — Florida's state-backed insurer — is actively issuing non-renewal notices in Sarasota County as its depopulation program continues. By Amanda LeGault | October 14, 2026

The Florida homeowners insurance market in 2026 looks nothing like what most buyers moving here expect. If you're coming from another state where insurance was an afterthought — something you figured out in the last week before closing — you're going to need to shift your timeline.

Insurance isn't optional. It's a hard closing requirement. Your lender won't fund the loan until you have a policy bound, paid, and in place. And in Sarasota County right now, finding that policy can be more complicated than it used to be.

Here's what you actually need to know.

The Citizens Insurance Situation

Citizens Property Insurance Corporation is Florida's state-backed insurer of last resort. For years, buyers who couldn't find private coverage — or found it too expensive — could fall back on Citizens. That fallback is getting harder to count on.

Citizens launched an aggressive depopulation program beginning in 2023, pushing policyholders to private carriers. The goal was to reduce the state's exposure, and it's working: Citizens has dropped from a peak of over 1.7 million policies statewide to below 800,000 as of 2026. Non-renewal notices are going out across Sarasota County.

What this means for buyers:

If the current owner has Citizens coverage, there's a good chance that policy won't transfer to you at closing — or that you won't be eligible to take it on. Citizens has specific eligibility requirements, and depopulation-assigned policies may shift to private carriers before or just after closing.

This isn't necessarily bad news. Many of the private carriers taking on former Citizens policies are offering competitive rates. But you need to know about it going in, not at the closing table.

Start shopping for insurance the same week you go under contract — ideally within the first week of your inspection period. Ask your agent whether the current owners are with Citizens, and if so, get your insurance agent involved immediately to identify private market options.

Four-Point Inspections: The Gate Most Buyers Don't See Coming

If you're buying a home that's 20 years old or older in Sarasota County — which covers a significant portion of the housing stock in neighborhoods like Gulf Gate, The Meadows, Venice, Laurel Park, and parts of Palmer Ranch — you will almost certainly need a four-point inspection before a private insurer will bind coverage.

A four-point inspection is separate from your standard home inspection. It's a shorter, more targeted assessment of four specific systems: roof, HVAC, plumbing, and electrical. Insurers use it to evaluate their risk on an older home before agreeing to cover it.

What passes and what fails

Roof: The insurer wants to know the age and condition. A roof with 5+ years of remaining useful life will typically pass. One that's at end of life, or with documented damage, won't — and your insurer won't bind coverage until it's replaced or until you negotiate with the seller to address it.

HVAC: Age and condition. Most insurers want functional systems. An old system that's still running but near the end of its expected lifespan will often still pass.

Plumbing: This is where surprises happen. Copper and CPVC plumbing pass. Polybutylene pipe — common in Florida homes built in the 1970s through mid-1990s — will cause most insurers to decline or surcharge heavily. If the home has poly pipe, you need to know before you make an offer, not after.

Electrical: Modern copper wiring and standard panels pass without issue. Federal Pacific, Zinsco, and Pushmatic panels are the three that routinely trigger declines or exclusions — they've been associated with fire risk and are still found in older Sarasota homes. Aluminum branch wiring can also be a flag for some carriers, though it's often insurable with specific exclusions or updates.

The four-point inspection typically costs $100–$200 and takes a couple of hours. Order it during your inspection period, not after. If something fails, you have time to negotiate with the seller, request a repair credit, or make an informed decision about whether to proceed.

Wind Mitigation: Where Buyers on the Gulf Coast Leave Money on the Table

A wind mitigation inspection is separate from the four-point, and many buyers skip it — which is a mistake, because it can meaningfully reduce your homeowners insurance premium.

Wind mitigation inspectors evaluate how your home is built to withstand hurricane-force winds. The credits depend on:

  • Roof shape — a hip roof (all four sides slope) earns the largest discount; a gable roof (two slopes) earns less
  • Roof-to-wall connection — how the roof is attached to the walls; hurricane straps earn significant credits
  • Roof deck attachment — how the decking is nailed to the framing
  • Opening protection — impact-resistant windows and doors, or shutters rated to Florida Building Code standards

On a $600,000 home in Sarasota County, wind mitigation credits can reduce your annual premium by $800–$2,500+ depending on the home's construction. Worth the $150 inspection fee.

Ask your seller for an existing wind mitigation report — many homeowners have one. If it's more than 5 years old, order a fresh one. Updated reports can unlock credits that an older report doesn't capture.

Premium Ranges: What to Budget

Sarasota County premiums vary significantly based on location, age, construction, and coverage elected. Here are honest ranges to use for your budget:

  • Single-family, $500K–$800K, Zone X: $3,000–$6,000/year for homeowners coverage alone
  • Single-family, $500K–$800K, Zone AE: $5,000–$12,000+/year when flood coverage is added (the Citizens flood mandate starting January 2027 adds another layer for Zone X buyers with Citizens policies)
  • Luxury, $1M–$3M+, Zone X: $6,000–$15,000+/year depending on construction and coverage elected
  • Condo (HO-6): Your HOA's master policy covers the building structure; you're insuring your interior, personal property, and liability. Budget $800–$2,000/year for a typical HO-6 policy — but always request the HOA's master policy certificate to confirm what it actually covers before you make an offer

These are ranges, not guarantees. Your specific number depends on the home's age, construction type, distance to water, flood zone, and what coverage limits and deductibles you elect. Wind deductibles in Florida are typically a percentage of dwelling coverage (2% is common), which can mean a $12,000–$16,000 deductible on a $600,000 home — factor that into your financial planning, not just your annual premium.

Getting at least two or three insurance quotes during your inspection period gives you real numbers to work with. Your Sarasota County closing costs already include title insurance (paid by the buyer here, unlike Pinellas County), lender fees, and prepaid items — insurance escrow adds to that, and it catches many buyers off guard.

The Timeline That Actually Works

Week 1 (as soon as you're under contract):

  • Contact your insurance agent and share the property address
  • Ask the listing agent: how old is the roof? What plumbing type? What electrical panel?
  • Request any existing four-point or wind mitigation reports from the seller

During inspection period:

  • Order the four-point inspection (if the home is 20+ years old)
  • Order the wind mitigation inspection
  • Have your insurance agent begin quoting with the four-point results in hand

2–3 weeks before closing:

  • Select and bind your policy
  • Pay the first year's premium (required at binding — not at closing)
  • Provide proof of insurance to your lender

What delays closings: A roof that fails the four-point, a plumbing type the insurer won't cover, or an electrical panel that triggers a decline. None of these are deal-killers on their own — but they all take time to resolve, and if you don't find them until two weeks before closing, you're in a difficult spot.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is homeowners insurance required to close on a home in Sarasota?

Yes, if you're financing the purchase. Your lender will require proof of a bound homeowners insurance policy before they fund the loan — you cannot close without it. Even cash buyers are strongly advised to secure coverage before taking title, since going without coverage after closing leaves you fully exposed to any loss from day one.

What is a four-point inspection and when do I need one in Florida?

A four-point inspection evaluates four systems — roof, HVAC, plumbing, and electrical — and is required by most Florida private insurers before they'll bind coverage on homes 20 years old or older. In Sarasota County, where significant housing stock was built in the 1970s through 2000s, buyers encounter this requirement frequently. The inspection costs $100–$200 and should be ordered during your inspection period, not after.

How does Citizens Insurance depopulation affect me as a Sarasota buyer?

Citizens is actively reducing its policy count statewide, including in Sarasota County. If the current owner has Citizens coverage, that policy may not transfer to you, or you may be assigned to a private carrier as part of Citizens' depopulation program. This doesn't mean you won't get coverage — many private carriers are competitive on rates — but it does mean you need to start shopping insurance early in your inspection period, not the week before closing.

What's the difference between a four-point inspection and a wind mitigation inspection?

A four-point inspection is for insurability — it tells the carrier whether the home's major systems meet their standards for coverage. A wind mitigation inspection is for credits — it tells the carrier how your home is built to withstand wind, which can reduce your annual premium. Both are typically ordered during the inspection period, both cost $100–$200, and both are worth doing on virtually any Sarasota County home purchase.

How much should I budget for homeowners insurance in Sarasota County?

In Zone X (most inland neighborhoods), budget $3,000–$6,000 per year for a single-family home in the $500K–$800K range. In Zone AE (barrier islands, waterfront areas), expect $5,000–$12,000+ when flood coverage is included. Luxury homes over $1M run $6,000–$15,000+ depending on construction and coverage elected. Condos typically need only an HO-6 policy — budget $800–$2,000 per year, but verify your HOA master policy coverage first.
