Manatee County Homestead Exemption: How to Apply After Closing and What the New $150,000 Exemption Saves You (2027)

How do I get the new homestead exemption on a Manatee County home I'm buying now?

To claim Florida's homestead exemption for 2027, you must own your Bradenton-area home and live in it as your permanent residence on January 1, 2027, then file with the Manatee County Property Appraiser by March 1, 2027. Because voters approved Amendment 3 in November 2026, the exemption against non-school property taxes rises to $150,000 for 2027 and $250,000 for 2028, up from the old $50,000. Close on December 31 and you qualify for 2027. Close on January 2 and you wait until 2028.

By Amanda LeGault, LeGault Luxe Group at Keller Williams on The Water Sarasota | November 26, 2026

Three weeks ago, the homestead exemption got a lot more valuable.

Florida voters passed Amendment 3 on November 3 with roughly 62% of the vote. If you're buying a primary home in Bradenton, Lakewood Ranch, Parrish, Ellenton, or Palmetto this winter, that changes the math on your property taxes, and it puts a hard date on your closing calendar.

Here's the thing most buyers miss: the exemption is not automatic. Your lender won't file it. Your title company won't file it. And the qualifying date is January 1, so a closing that slips by a few days into the new year can cost you a full year of savings.

Here's exactly how it works in Manatee County, what it's worth, and how to file.

What Changed With Amendment 3, and What It's Worth in Manatee County

Under the old rules, Florida's homestead exemption took up to $50,000 off your assessed value: $25,000 against all taxes, plus another $25,000 against non-school taxes.

Amendment 3 expands the part that applies to non-school taxes (county, city, fire district, and other local levies):

  • 2027 tax year: up to $150,000 exempt from non-school taxes
  • 2028 tax year and beyond: up to $250,000, adjusted for inflation going forward
  • School taxes: the $25,000 school-tax portion stays the same

What does that mean in dollars? Your actual number depends on your taxing district. A home in unincorporated Parrish, inside the City of Bradenton, and on Anna Maria Island all carry different millage rates. To keep the math simple, here's an illustration using 8 mills of non-school millage (every 1 mill is $1 per $1,000 of taxable value):

With Manatee County's typical home value sitting around $409,000 (Zillow, mid-2026), most primary-residence buyers will use the full exemption. On a $450,000 Lakewood Ranch resale or a $425,000 Parrish new build, that's real money every single year you own the home.

The December 31 closing question

Florida homestead runs on a single snapshot date: January 1. To qualify for 2027, you need to:

  • Hold title to the home (deed recorded), and
  • Actually live there as your permanent residence on January 1, 2027

If you're under contract right now on a Bradenton home, this is worth a conversation with your lender and title company. Pushing a closing from January 5 back to December 30 can be the difference between ~$1,200 in savings next year and $0. It's one of the first things I check on every contract that closes this time of year.

The same applies to new construction. If your Parrish or Lakewood Ranch builder is projecting a late-December completion, ask now about the realistic certificate of occupancy date. A builder delay into January pushes your first exemption year to 2028.

How to Apply With the Manatee County Property Appraiser

Step 1: Lock in residency by January 1

Close, move in, and make the home your permanent residence. The Property Appraiser looks at where you actually live, not just what you own.

Step 2: Update your Florida documents

If you're relocating from out of state, get these updated with your Manatee County address as soon as possible after closing:

  • Florida driver's license or Florida ID card
  • Florida vehicle registration
  • Manatee County voter registration (if you're a U.S. citizen)
  • Permanent resident card, if you're not a U.S. citizen

You'll also need the Social Security numbers for you and your spouse. Florida law requires them on the application.

Step 3: File online or in person

File online through the Manatee County Property Appraiser's website (manateepao.gov), or in person at the main office, 915 4th Ave. W., Bradenton. Check the site for when filing opens for the 2027 tax year, and file as early as you're eligible rather than waiting for the deadline.

Step 4: Hit March 1, 2027, then check your TRIM notice

The deadline is March 1, 2027. In August, you'll receive your TRIM (Truth in Millage) notice. Confirm the homestead exemption shows on it. If it doesn't, call the Property Appraiser right away.

Missed March 1? Florida allows a late application, generally within 25 days after TRIM notices are mailed, but approval isn't automatic. Don't plan on it.

What Buyers Get Wrong at the Closing Table

Your first-year tax bill isn't the seller's tax bill. At closing, property taxes are prorated based on the seller's current bill, which may include their homestead exemption and years of Save Our Homes protection. After a sale, Manatee County reassesses the home at market value. Your 2027 bill can be meaningfully higher than what you saw on the listing, which is exactly why getting your own exemption on file matters.

Save Our Homes starts the year after your exemption begins. Once your homestead is in place, annual increases in your assessed value are capped at 3% or the change in CPI, whichever is lower. In a flat-to-soft market like Manatee County right now, that cap may not bite in year one, but over 10 years of ownership it typically builds a large gap between market value and taxable value.

Selling and buying within Florida? Use portability. If you're a seller moving from a long-held West Bradenton or Palma Sola home to a Lakewood Ranch or Ellenton home, you can transfer up to $500,000 of your Save Our Homes benefit to the new homestead. Request it on the same application. You generally have three tax years after leaving your old homestead to claim it, but there's no reason to wait.

Second homes don't qualify, but they still benefit. If you're buying on Anna Maria Island, Holmes Beach, Bradenton Beach, or the north end of Longboat Key as a vacation or rental property, you can't claim homestead. Amendment 3 does lower the annual assessment-increase cap on non-homestead property from 10% to 5% starting January 1, 2027. Frequently Asked Questions

When is the homestead exemption deadline in Manatee County?

March 1, 2027 for the 2027 tax year. You must own the home and live in it as your permanent residence on January 1, 2027, and file with the Manatee County Property Appraiser by March 1. Filing late generally pushes the exemption to the following year.

If I close on a Bradenton home in December 2026, do I get the new $150,000 exemption?

Yes, as long as the deed is recorded and you are living in the home as your permanent residence on January 1, 2027, and you file by March 1, 2027. Close on January 2, 2027 instead and you will not qualify until the 2028 tax year.

How much does the new homestead exemption save in Manatee County?

Under Amendment 3, the exemption against non-school taxes rises to $150,000 for 2027 and $250,000 for 2028, while the $25,000 school-tax portion stays the same. At an illustrative 8 mills of non-school millage, the extra $100,000 of exemption is worth about $800 a year in 2027 compared with the old rules. Your exact savings depend on your taxing district.

Can I get a homestead exemption on an Anna Maria Island or Longboat Key second home?

No. The homestead exemption only applies to your permanent Florida residence, and vacation rentals and second homes do not qualify. Under Amendment 3, non-homestead properties do get a lower annual assessment increase cap of 5% starting in 2027, down from 10%.

Can I move my Save Our Homes savings to a new home in Manatee County?

Yes. Florida portability lets you transfer up to $500,000 of your Save Our Homes benefit from a prior Florida homestead to your new one. You apply on the same homestead application with the Manatee County Property Appraiser, and you generally have three tax years after leaving your prior homestead to claim it.

The bottom line: the new homestead exemption is one of the biggest property-tax breaks Florida homeowners have ever had, but only if you're in the home on January 1 and you file by March 1. If you're buying in Bradenton or anywhere in Manatee County this winter, your closing date is now a tax decision.
