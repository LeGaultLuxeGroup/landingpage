How Much Does It Cost to Sell a House in Sarasota, FL? (2026 Net Proceeds Guide)

How much does it cost to sell a house in Sarasota, FL in 2026?

Selling a home in Sarasota, FL typically costs between 7–9% of the sale price, covering listing agent commission, documentary stamp taxes ($0.70 per $100), home preparation, prorated property taxes, and HOA fees. One critical Sarasota distinction: the buyer pays for title insurance here — not the seller — which saves Sarasota sellers $3,000–$6,500 compared to sellers in Pinellas County or most other parts of Florida. On a $900,000 Sarasota home, total selling costs typically land between $63,000–$81,000, leaving a net of roughly $819,000–$837,000 before mortgage payoff.

The Cost to Sell a House in Sarasota — Line by Line

Some of these costs are fixed by Florida law. Others depend on how you negotiate, how the market behaves, and what condition your home is in. Let's take them in order.

1. Real Estate Agent Commission

Since the NAR settlement took full effect in August 2024, listing agent commission and buyer's agent compensation are handled separately — and negotiated independently.

Your listing agent fee typically runs 2.5–3% of the sale price. Buyer's agent compensation is now negotiated separately, and whether you offer it — and how much — is your call as the seller. Many Sarasota sellers still offer it (typically 2–2.5%) as a seller concession to maximize buyer-side activity on their listing.

All-in, most sellers working with a full-service agent and offering buyer's agent compensation pay 4.5–5.5% of the sale price. If your buyer negotiates their own compensation directly, your cost drops to 2.5–3%.

On a $1,000,000 Sarasota home at 5%, that's $50,000 in combined commission.

2. Documentary Stamp Tax (Doc Stamps)

Florida requires sellers to pay documentary stamp taxes on the deed at closing. In Sarasota County, the rate is $0.70 per $100 of the sale price — exactly 0.70% — and it applies to the full sale price without exception.

3. Title Insurance — Here's Where Sarasota Is Different

This surprises almost every seller who's moved from out of state — or who's sold property in Pinellas County.

In Sarasota County, the buyer pays for title insurance, not the seller. This is the opposite of what happens in Pinellas County and what most national real estate websites will tell you. It's a regional custom, and it meaningfully changes your cost picture.

What does this save you? Owner's title insurance premiums in Florida are filed rates. On a $900,000 sale, the policy runs approximately $3,800–$4,500. On a $1.5M sale, closer to $5,500–$6,500. As a Sarasota seller, none of that comes out of your proceeds — it comes out of the buyer's.

You still pay for the lender's title policy on any mortgage you're paying off — but that's a much smaller fee, typically $500–$800, paid to release the existing lien.

4. Home Preparation and Pre-Listing Costs

This is the cost item that varies most between sellers — and the one most directly tied to your sale price and days on market.

At minimum, expect to spend on:

  • Deep cleaning and landscaping: $500–$2,000
  • Minor repairs (items that will appear on a buyer's inspection): $1,000–$5,000
  • Staging (partial staging is the baseline for Sarasota's $750K+ segment): $1,500–$5,000

Well-prepared homes in Sarasota's luxury price range sell faster and closer to list price. In my experience, the sellers who skip prep spend more time on market and net the same or less than if they'd spent a few thousand dollars upfront. The math usually works in favor of preparation.

5. Prorated Property Taxes

Florida property taxes are paid in arrears, meaning you owe a prorated share of the current year's taxes from January 1 through your closing date. The buyer receives a credit for that amount at closing.

Sarasota County's millage rate varies depending on your municipality, whether your property has a homestead exemption, and the Sarasota County Property Appraiser's assessed value. For non-homesteaded properties — second homes, investment properties, recently purchased homes — the tax bill is higher and the proration credit more significant.

On a non-homesteaded $1,000,000 property in Sarasota, annual taxes commonly run $10,000–$16,000. Closing in October means crediting the buyer approximately 75–80% of the annual tax at closing.

6. HOA Fees, Estoppel Letters, and Condo-Specific Costs

If your property sits in an HOA, condominium association, or master-planned community — Palmer Ranch, The Meadows, Lakewood Ranch's Sarasota section, downtown Sarasota condos — you'll face association-related closing costs.

Estoppel letters (official statements of dues owed, violations, and any pending special assessments) typically cost $150–$500 per association. Prepaid HOA dues prorate at closing as well.

For condo sellers specifically: buyers in Sarasota are asking for full documentation of milestone inspections under Florida's SB 4D requirements before they write offers on any building over 30 years old. Make sure you have those records — or know your association does. The gap in documentation creates delays. (Read more about what Sarasota condo buyers are scrutinizing before closing.)

7. Seller Concessions (Negotiated, Market-Dependent)

In the current Sarasota market, buyer concessions have become a real negotiating lever — especially for properties that require more time to find the right buyer.

Common concessions include closing cost credits and interest rate buydowns (2-1 buydowns and permanent rate buydowns in particular). If a buyer asks for a 1.5% concession on a $1M home, that's $15,000 out of your net. Not every seller faces this — well-priced properties in strong pockets still move competitively — but it belongs in a realistic range.

Frequently Asked Questions

What fees does a seller pay at closing in Sarasota, FL?

Sarasota sellers typically pay listing agent commission (2.5–3%), buyer's agent compensation if offered (1.5–2.5%), documentary stamp taxes ($0.70 per $100 of sale price), prorated property taxes, HOA estoppel fees, and home preparation costs. Unlike sellers in most Florida counties, Sarasota sellers do not pay for the buyer's title insurance policy — that cost falls to the buyer in Sarasota County, often saving sellers $3,000–$6,500.

Does the seller pay title insurance in Sarasota County?

No. In Sarasota County, the buyer customarily pays for the owner's title insurance policy — the opposite of convention in Pinellas County and what many national real estate guides assume. This saves Sarasota sellers $3,000–$6,500 compared to what a seller would pay in Clearwater or St. Petersburg on a comparable-priced home.

How much are documentary stamp taxes on a Florida home sale?

Doc stamps in Florida are $0.70 per $100 of the sale price (0.70%) and are paid by the seller. On a $1,000,000 sale, that's $7,000. On a $500,000 sale, it's $3,500. This is a fixed Florida law requirement — it cannot be negotiated away, though you can factor it into your pricing and net proceeds calculation.

Do I owe capital gains tax when I sell my Sarasota home?

If the home was your primary residence for at least two of the last five years, you may exclude up to $250,000 in gain ($500,000 for married couples) from federal capital gains tax. Florida has no state income tax, so capital gains are a federal-only concern. If your gain exceeds the exclusion limits — possible at Sarasota's price points — work with a CPA before closing to understand your exposure.

What is the best time of year to sell a house in Sarasota, FL?

Sarasota's peak selling season runs January through April, driven by snowbird traffic and out-of-state buyers who concentrate activity in the winter months. That said, the luxury segment is active year-round, and October and November offer a real window for sellers targeting buyers who want to close before year-end. Q3 2026 RASM data showed inventory tightening into fall — a favorable signal for current sellers.
