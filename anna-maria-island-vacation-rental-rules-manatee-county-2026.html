Anna Maria Island Vacation Rental Rules: What Manatee County's 2026 Ordinance Means Before You Buy

What are the current vacation rental rules on Anna Maria Island, and what is Manatee County proposing to change?

Short-term rentals are currently permitted on Anna Maria Island, but rules vary by municipality — Anna Maria, Holmes Beach, and Bradenton Beach each have their own STR ordinances. Manatee County commissioners are actively drafting a new county-wide STR ordinance in 2026 that could include density caps and stricter registration requirements, with officials publicly split on the approach and a vote expected before year's end. Buyers purchasing investment properties on AMI right now face real transaction risk if they close without understanding both the current regulatory framework and what could change before they collect their first rental check.

The Current Landscape: It's Not One Set of Rules

Anna Maria Island sits in Manatee County, but "AMI" isn't a single regulatory jurisdiction. The island is home to three incorporated municipalities — Anna Maria City, Holmes Beach, and Bradenton Beach — each operating under its own city government and, importantly, its own short-term rental ordinance. Properties in unincorporated Manatee County territory follow county-level rules.

That means the parcel you're buying controls which rulebook applies. A property on Gulf Drive in Holmes Beach and a property in unincorporated county territory a mile away can have entirely different STR licensing requirements, occupancy standards, and operating restrictions.

Before you make an offer, your first question should be: which jurisdiction governs this parcel? Your agent can confirm this, but you'll want to verify it yourself with the Manatee County Property Appraiser's office, which maintains parcel data online at pamanatee.org.

What Each AMI City Currently Requires

Each of the three AMI cities has enacted STR registration and licensing programs. Generally, these frameworks include:

  • Annual STR license or registration with the city
  • Occupancy limits (typically two persons per bedroom, with a maximum cap)
  • Minimum rental duration requirements (varies by city — some have minimum 7-day requirements)
  • Noise, parking, and trash management rules
  • Local contact/property manager requirements for response to complaints
  • Inspection or safety certification requirements

The details matter enormously. If you're buying a four-bedroom property and assuming you can sleep twelve guests, you need to confirm the specific occupancy rules for that city — not the general framework. These limits directly affect your revenue projections.

Florida State Law: Why This Is More Complicated Than Most States

Florida has one of the most contested vacation rental regulatory landscapes in the country. The state has repeatedly amended its preemption laws — the rules about how much authority local governments have to restrict STRs beyond state licensing standards.

The short version: Florida law generally prevents local governments from banning short-term rentals outright or imposing restrictions so severe they function as a de facto ban. But municipalities and counties can regulate operational elements — noise, parking, occupancy safety, registration — as long as they stay within boundaries established by state law.

Where it gets complicated is the grandfathering issue. Local ordinances adopted before certain state preemption deadlines may have different legal standing than newer rules. And this area of law has been actively litigated across Florida — meaning what's allowed today could be clarified or overturned by a court ruling tomorrow.

This legal complexity is one reason the Manatee County debate has been so charged. Commissioners who want tighter restrictions are working within a framework that limits how far they can go. Those opposing new restrictions argue the current rules are already working. The outcome of their vote will be shaped as much by legal counsel as by political preference.

What the Proposed Manatee County Ordinance Could Change

Based on reporting through mid-2026, the draft ordinance under consideration by Manatee County could include:

  • Density caps — limiting the total number of STR licenses issued in a given area or per parcel type, which could effectively freeze new investment properties out of the STR market if the cap is already met
  • Enhanced registration requirements — including inspections, safety certifications, and renewal conditions more rigorous than current standards
  • Stricter operational rules — tighter occupancy formulas, noise ordinance enforcement with fines, and mandatory local agent contact response times
  • Increased penalty structures — higher fines for violations that could make operating outside the rules economically non-viable

What no one knows yet is the final form. Commissioners have been public about their disagreement, and ordinances often look significantly different between the draft stage and the final vote. There's also the question of whether the cities on AMI — Anna Maria, Holmes Beach, Bradenton Beach — will feel pressure to update their own frameworks in response to whatever the county adopts.

This is exactly the kind of regulatory uncertainty that should be named explicitly in your purchase offer.

How This Affects Your Financing

If you're financing an AMI investment property using projected rental income — which is common at the $1M–$5M+ price points that dominate the island — your lender is making underwriting decisions based on that income picture.

DSCR loans (Debt Service Coverage Ratio loans) are popular for STR investment properties in Florida's coastal markets. These loans qualify based on the property's income rather than your personal income, using market rent estimates or actual lease agreements. If regulatory uncertainty clouds the rental income projection, lenders may:

  • Require confirmation of current STR licensing eligibility before closing
  • Apply a risk discount to projected income, requiring a larger down payment to hit coverage ratios
  • Ask for a legal opinion on the property's STR status under current and pending rules
  • In some cases, decline to underwrite based on STR income if the licensing picture is unclear

Tell your lender about the pending ordinance early. Don't wait for them to find it. A lender who understands Florida coastal STR financing — and there are good ones who specialize in this — will be able to structure the deal appropriately. One who doesn't will create last-minute problems.

Due Diligence Before You Close

This isn't a situation where you do your research after going under contract. Here's the list of questions to resolve before you make an offer — or at minimum, before your inspection period expires:

  1. Which jurisdiction governs this parcel? Confirm with the Manatee County Property Appraiser. Don't assume based on the street address or neighborhood name.
  2. Does the property currently have a valid STR license or registration? Ask for documentation. Confirm it's current, not lapsed.
  3. What does the seller's rental history show? Request actual occupancy and income data — not just projections. Year-over-year comparisons reveal whether revenue has been growing or declining as regulations have tightened.
  4. Are there HOA or condo restrictions on rentals? Some AMI condominiums have governing documents that limit or prohibit short-term rentals regardless of what city or county rules allow. The condo docs control — and you're entitled to review them during your due diligence period.
  5. Does the property require a SB 4D milestone inspection? If you're buying a condominium building that is three or more stories and 25 or more years old, Florida's SB 4D milestone inspection requirements apply. Confirm the building's inspection status before closing — and factor any required structural remediation costs into your offer.
  6. What does local legal counsel say? For a $1M+ AMI investment, a one-hour consultation with a Florida real estate attorney who tracks Manatee County ordinance developments is not optional. It's a cost of doing this transaction correctly.

Your purchase contract can also include specific language around regulatory contingencies. If the county ordinance passes in a form that materially restricts the property's STR eligibility, a well-drafted contract gives you an exit — or a price renegotiation — rather than leaving you holding a property that doesn't perform as projected.

Frequently Asked Questions

Are vacation rentals currently allowed on Anna Maria Island?

Yes, short-term rentals are currently permitted on Anna Maria Island, but the rules differ by jurisdiction. The three incorporated cities on the island — Anna Maria, Holmes Beach, and Bradenton Beach — each have their own STR ordinances covering registration, licensing, occupancy limits, noise, and parking. Properties in unincorporated Manatee County areas follow county-level rules. Before purchasing, you need to confirm which municipality governs the specific parcel and what that municipality currently allows.

What is Manatee County proposing in its 2026 vacation rental ordinance?

As of mid-2026, Manatee County commissioners directed staff to draft a comprehensive short-term rental ordinance that could include density caps, enhanced registration and inspection requirements, stricter occupancy and parking standards, and increased penalties for violations. The proposed rules would primarily affect unincorporated county areas, though the debate has raised questions about whether the incorporated cities on Anna Maria Island might strengthen their own rules in response. A commission vote was expected in late 2026, but the outcome remained uncertain with commissioners publicly split on the approach.

How does Florida state law affect what Manatee County can regulate?

Florida has historically preempted local governments from banning short-term rentals outright or restricting them in ways that exceed state licensing standards. However, state law has evolved and generally allows counties and cities to regulate operational matters — noise, parking, trash, occupancy safety, and registration — as long as they're not imposing a de facto ban. Local ordinances adopted before certain state preemption deadlines may also have grandfathered protections. The legal landscape is complex and actively litigated in Florida courts, which is exactly why you need a buyer's agent who tracks these shifts in real time for Manatee County specifically.

Will a pending ordinance affect my mortgage approval for an Anna Maria Island investment property?

It can. Lenders who use projected short-term rental income to qualify buyers — through DSCR loans or investment property financing programs — base those projections on the property's current rental-eligible status and historical occupancy. If a new ordinance creates regulatory uncertainty, some lenders may require confirmation of licensing eligibility before closing, reduce their projected income estimates, or flag the property as higher risk. Buyers should disclose the pending ordinance to their lender early and work with a lender who understands Florida STR financing in coastal markets.

What due diligence should I do before buying an investment property on Anna Maria Island right now?

At minimum: confirm which municipality governs the parcel, verify the property's current STR license or registration status, request the seller's historical rental income and occupancy data, review the HOA or condo docs for rental restrictions, confirm whether the property has a valid SB 4D milestone inspection (if applicable to condo buildings), and consult with a local attorney who tracks Manatee County and AMI city ordinance developments. Your agent should also help you draft contract language that addresses regulatory contingencies — protecting your earnest money if the ordinance passes and materially changes the rental picture before you close.
