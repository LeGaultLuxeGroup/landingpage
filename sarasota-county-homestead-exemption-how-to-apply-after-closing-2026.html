Sarasota County Homestead Exemption: How to Apply After Closing and What You'll Save on Property Taxes (2026)

What is the homestead exemption deadline in Sarasota County, and how do I apply after closing?

Florida's homestead exemption reduces your property's taxable assessed value by up to $50,000 — saving most Sarasota County homeowners $600–$1,100 or more per year in property taxes, depending on your millage rate. To claim it on your 2027 tax bill, you must file with the Sarasota County Property Appraiser by March 1, 2027. Apply online at sarasotapropertyappraiser.com using your deed, Florida driver's license, and Florida vehicle registration or voter registration card as proof of primary residency.

By Amanda LeGault, LeGault Luxe Group at Keller Williams | November 4, 2026

You made it to closing. The keys are in your hand, the boxes are being unpacked, and you're finally settling into your new Sarasota home.

Here's the thing most buyers don't realize until weeks — or months — later: your property tax situation isn't finished yet.

Florida gives homeowners a significant property tax benefit called the homestead exemption. It can save you hundreds or even thousands of dollars every year. But it's not automatic. No one mails you an application. Your lender won't remind you. Your title company has moved on to the next closing.

If you closed on a Sarasota home in 2026 and plan to live there as your primary residence, you need to file with the Sarasota County Property Appraiser by March 1, 2027 to get the exemption applied to your 2027 tax bill.

Miss that deadline, and you wait a full year to try again.

Here's everything you need to know to file, what you'll save, and why the savings compound significantly over time.

What the Homestead Exemption Actually Does

Florida's homestead exemption works in two layers, and together they reduce the assessed value of your home for property tax purposes by up to $50,000.

The first $25,000 comes off your assessed value and applies to all taxing authorities — including schools, the county, and your municipality.

The second $25,000 applies to assessed value between $50,000 and $75,000, exempting that portion from all taxes except school district levies. In practice, the second $25,000 saves you a bit less than the first.

What does that translate to in real dollars?

It depends on your millage rate — the combined rate of every taxing authority covering your property. In unincorporated Sarasota County, total millage typically runs around 11–12 mills. Inside the City of Sarasota, the combined rate is higher, usually 15–16 mills.

  • At 12 mills, a $50,000 reduction in assessed value saves you around $600 per year
  • At 15.5 mills, that same exemption saves you around $775 per year

That's the base exemption alone. Save Our Homes — which I'll cover next — is where the real long-term value lives.

How to Apply with the Sarasota County Property Appraiser

The Sarasota County Property Appraiser's office makes it straightforward to file online.

Step 1: Gather your documents

You'll need proof that your Sarasota property is your primary Florida residence as of January 1, 2027 — that's the qualifying date for the 2027 tax year. Acceptable documents include:

  • Florida driver's license or state ID with your Sarasota address
  • Florida vehicle registration showing your Sarasota address
  • Sarasota County voter registration card (if applicable)
  • Your recorded deed for the property

If you relocated from out of state, you'll want to get your Florida driver's license and vehicle registration updated before you file. The Property Appraiser needs to see that Florida is your primary state of residence — not just a property you own.

Step 2: File online at sarasotapropertyappraiser.com

The Property Appraiser's office accepts online applications at sarasotapropertyappraiser.com. You'll create an account, search for your property by address or parcel number, and submit your application with supporting documents.

If you prefer to file in person, the main office is in downtown Sarasota. Satellite locations and office hours are listed on the Property Appraiser's website.

Step 3: File by March 1, 2027

The deadline is firm. Applications submitted after March 1, 2027 will apply to the 2028 tax year — not 2027. You cannot backdate a homestead exemption.

If you think you qualified for the 2026 tax year but missed that deadline, the Property Appraiser's office does accept late filing petitions. They're reviewed case by case and aren't guaranteed to be approved, but it's worth filing if you have a reasonable explanation for missing the deadline.

Save Our Homes: Where the Savings Really Compound

This is the part most buyers don't understand until year two or three.

Once your homestead exemption is in place, Florida law limits how much your property's assessed value can increase each year. Under the Save Our Homes cap, your assessed value can rise by no more than 3% or the rate of inflation — whichever is lower.

Your market value might jump 10% or 15% in a strong Sarasota year. Your taxable assessed value? Capped at 3%.

Here's what that looks like for a home with an initial assessed value of $700,000:

  • Year 1 (at purchase): Assessed value = $700,000
  • Year 2 (3% SOH cap): Assessed value = $721,000 — even if market value climbs to $780,000
  • Year 5 (compounded): Assessed value ≈ $810,000 — even if market value reaches $900,000+
  • Year 10: Assessed value ≈ $940,000 — even if the home is worth $1.1M+

That growing gap between market value and assessed value is your SOH differential — money you're not paying taxes on. At 12 mills, every additional $100,000 that sits above your assessed value saves you $1,200 per year in property taxes.

Homeowners who've owned Sarasota homes for 10 or 15 years often have assessed values dramatically below what their home could sell for today. That's the compounding power of Save Our Homes, and it starts the moment your homestead is active. The sooner you file, the sooner the cap begins working for you.

Property Tax Portability: Moving from Another Florida Home?

If you're selling a Florida primary residence to buy in Sarasota, pay close attention to this — it's one of the most underutilized benefits in Florida real estate.

Florida's portability provision lets you transfer your accumulated SOH differential from your prior home to your new Sarasota property — up to a maximum of $500,000.

Instead of starting from zero on a new Sarasota home's assessed value, you can effectively carry forward years of compounding Save Our Homes savings to reduce your new home's assessed value from day one. For long-time Florida homeowners with significant SOH differentials, portability can mean tens of thousands of dollars in annual tax savings on the new property.

To claim portability, apply through the Sarasota County Property Appraiser's office at the same time you file your homestead exemption. You typically have three tax years after the year you gave up your prior homestead to claim portability — so don't wait if this applies to you.

Bring documentation of your prior home's assessed value and SOH differential to your exemption filing. The Property Appraiser's staff can walk you through the calculation and confirm how much you can transfer.

Additional Exemptions Worth Knowing

The standard homestead exemption is the starting point. Depending on your situation, you may qualify for additional reductions that apply on top of it:

  • Senior citizen exemption: An additional exemption for Sarasota County residents 65 or older who meet annual income requirements. Thresholds are adjusted each year — check the Property Appraiser's site for current limits.
  • Widow or widower exemption: An additional $500 off your assessed value.
  • Disability exemption: Ranges from $500 to full exemption depending on disability classification.
  • Veterans and first responder exemptions: Multiple tiers based on service status and disability rating — some are very significant.

Each requires a separate application and documentation. The Property Appraiser's office can help you identify which ones apply to your situation.

A Note on Florida's Amendment 3 (November 2026 Ballot)

Florida voters considered an expansion of the homestead exemption on the November 2026 ballot (HJR 1F, known on the ballot as Amendment 3). If it passed, the amendment could significantly increase the base exemption for qualifying homeowners, with additional savings potentially beginning in a future tax year.

Since this post was published during election week, check the Sarasota County Supervisor of Elections or the Florida Secretary of State's site for certified results. If the amendment passed, the Sarasota County Property Appraiser's office will have guidance on how any new exemption amounts affect current and pending applications.

Frequently Asked Questions

When is the homestead exemption deadline in Sarasota County?

The deadline is March 1 of each year. For the 2027 tax year, you must have your application submitted with the Sarasota County Property Appraiser by March 1, 2027. Missing the deadline means your exemption won't apply until the following tax year — there's no proration or partial-year credit.

Can I apply for the homestead exemption online in Sarasota County?

Yes. The Sarasota County Property Appraiser's office accepts online applications at sarasotapropertyappraiser.com. You'll need to upload proof of Florida residency — typically your Florida driver's license or state ID with your Sarasota address, along with your Florida vehicle registration or voter registration card.

What happens if I miss the homestead exemption deadline?

If you miss March 1, you can file a late application petition with the Property Appraiser's office. Late petitions are reviewed individually and are not guaranteed to be approved. In most cases, missing the deadline means your exemption will begin in the following tax year — a buyer who closes in 2026 and misses March 1, 2027 would first see the exemption applied in 2028.

How does Florida property tax portability work for Sarasota buyers?

If you owned a Florida homestead prior to purchasing your Sarasota home, you can transfer your accumulated Save Our Homes differential — up to $500,000 — to your new property. Apply for portability at the same time you file your homestead exemption with the Sarasota County Property Appraiser. You generally have three tax years after abandoning your prior homestead to claim it.

Does the homestead exemption apply to condos and townhomes in Sarasota?

Yes. The Florida homestead exemption applies to any property used as your primary Florida residence — single-family homes, condos, townhomes, and mobile homes all qualify. The same March 1 deadline and online application process apply regardless of property type.

The homestead exemption is one of those things that doesn't come up at the closing table — but it matters for every year you own your Sarasota home. The base savings are meaningful. The Save Our Homes cap compounds significantly over time. And if you're carrying a portability benefit from a prior Florida home, filing correctly on day one could protect tens of thousands of dollars in tax advantages.

Don't let the March 1 deadline sneak past you. If you relocated from out of state, get your Florida driver's license and vehicle registration updated and file online at sarasotapropertyappraiser.com well before the deadline.
