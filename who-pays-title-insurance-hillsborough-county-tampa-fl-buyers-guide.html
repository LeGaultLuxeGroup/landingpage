Who Pays Title Insurance in Hillsborough County, FL? A Tampa Buyer's Guide to Closing Costs (2026)

Who pays title insurance when buying a home in Hillsborough County (Tampa), FL?

In Hillsborough County, the buyer typically pays for both the owner's title insurance policy and the lender's title insurance policy at closing — unlike in some other Florida counties where the seller customarily covers the owner's policy. This local convention is not a law; it can be negotiated in the purchase contract. Florida title insurance rates are state-promulgated, meaning the premium is the same regardless of which title company you choose. On a $600,000 Tampa purchase, buyers should budget approximately $3,200–$3,800 for the combined title insurance cost, in addition to lender fees, intangible tax on the mortgage, and recording fees.

Here's the closing cost question I get almost every week from buyers who are under contract on a Tampa home: "Wait — I have to pay for title insurance? I thought that was the seller's job."

It's one of the most consistent surprises in a Tampa purchase, and it happens because title insurance customs vary across Florida counties in ways that no loan estimate form explains. If you came from a Pinellas County transaction, or you're relocating from out of state, the Hillsborough County convention can add a few thousand dollars to your cash-to-close that you weren't expecting.

Here's exactly how it works — and what your total closing cost picture looks like for a typical Tampa purchase.

The Hillsborough County Title Insurance Convention

Florida doesn't have a statewide law that dictates who pays for title insurance. Instead, each county developed its own prevailing custom over decades, and those customs stuck. In Hillsborough County, the buyer customarily pays for both title insurance policies at closing: the owner's policy (which protects your ownership interest in the property) and the lender's policy (which your mortgage lender requires as a condition of the loan).

This is different from, say, Pinellas County — where the seller more often pays for the owner's title policy. If you've bought in St. Pete or Clearwater, or if your Realtor works across multiple markets without flagging this, you can end up genuinely surprised when your preliminary closing disclosure arrives.

The most important thing to understand: this convention is negotiable. The standard Florida Realtors/Florida Bar contracts allow buyers and sellers to agree on any allocation of title costs. In practice, whether you can negotiate a seller contribution to title depends heavily on market conditions — in a competitive multiple-offer situation, asking the seller to cover title costs can weaken your offer. In a slower market, it's a reasonable ask.

What Title Insurance Actually Costs in Tampa

Florida is one of about a dozen states where title insurance rates are "promulgated" — set by the state's Department of Financial Services. Every licensed title company charges the same rate for the same transaction; you can't comparison-shop the premium. What varies between title companies is their closing fees, service quality, and turnaround time.

Florida's simultaneous issue rate applies when both policies are purchased together (which is almost always the case). Here's what buyers typically pay at current rates on Tampa transactions:

  • $500,000 purchase: approximately $2,800–$3,200 (combined owner's + lender's)
  • $700,000 purchase: approximately $3,500–$4,000
  • $900,000 purchase: approximately $4,600–$5,200
  • $1,200,000 purchase: approximately $5,800–$6,500
  • $1,500,000 purchase: approximately $7,000–$7,800

These are one-time costs. Title insurance has no annual premium — you pay once at closing, and the coverage lasts as long as you own the property (for the owner's policy) or until the loan is paid off (for the lender's policy).

Note that the title company's closing fee — separate from the insurance premium — also varies. Tampa title companies typically charge $400–$900 in closing/settlement fees on top of the insurance premium. This is negotiable and worth asking about when you choose a title company.

The Full Buyer Closing Cost Picture in Hillsborough County

Title insurance is the biggest variable in Tampa buyer closing costs — but it's not the only one. Here's how a typical closing looks for a Hillsborough County buyer in the $600K–$900K range:

Buyer-paid costs at closing (estimates on a $700,000 purchase with 20% down / $560,000 mortgage):

  • Title insurance (owner's + lender's, simultaneous issue): ~$3,500–$4,000
  • Intangible tax on mortgage: $1,120 ($0.002 × $560,000)
  • Recording fees (deed + mortgage): ~$200–$300
  • Lender origination/underwriting fees: $1,500–$3,000 (varies by lender)
  • Appraisal: $600–$900
  • Prepaid homeowners insurance (12 months): $3,000–$8,000+ (varies significantly by location and flood zone)
  • Prepaid property taxes (escrow setup): 2–6 months depending on closing date
  • Mortgage interest prepaid (to end of month): varies
  • HOA estoppel fee (if applicable): $150–$500

Typical total buyer closing costs in Hillsborough County run 2.5%–4% of the purchase price, not including your down payment. On a $700,000 home, that's $17,500–$28,000 in closing costs on top of the $140,000 down payment (at 20%).

Seller-paid costs work differently. The seller typically pays:

  • Documentary stamp tax on the deed: $0.70 per $100 of sale price. On a $700,000 sale, that's $4,900.
  • Real estate commissions
  • Prorated property taxes
  • Any agreed-upon seller concessions

Doc stamps are one of the largest seller closing costs and a frequent point of confusion — buyers sometimes think they owe doc stamps too, but in Florida the deed tax is a seller obligation. The buyer pays the intangible tax on their mortgage note, which is a separate line item.

How to Use This Information in Your Offer Strategy

Understanding who customarily pays what in Hillsborough County gives you a few strategic options when making an offer:

Request seller concessions toward closing costs. In a balanced or buyer-favoring market, it's common to ask the seller to contribute a flat dollar amount toward buyer closing costs. A $10,000–$15,000 seller concession can offset a meaningful portion of your title insurance and lender fees — effectively rolling some of your closing costs into the financed amount while reducing your cash-to-close.

Negotiate a seller-paid owner's title policy. Asking the seller to pay for just the owner's title policy (rather than the lender's) is a more targeted ask that follows Pinellas County custom rather than Hillsborough's. Some sellers in competitive South Tampa or Hyde Park won't consider it. Others will. Your agent's read on the seller's motivations matters here.

Choose your title company strategically. Your lender may recommend a title company, but you have the right to select your own in Florida. Ask about their closing/settlement fees, which are the negotiable part. A responsive, experienced title company also matters in competitive transactions where speed counts.

Every closing is a little different — the specific numbers depend on your loan type, your lender's fees, the closing date, and what's been negotiated in the contract. How much does title insurance cost for a buyer in Tampa, FL?

On a $600,000 purchase, the simultaneous issue rate for both the owner's and lender's policies typically runs approximately $3,200–$3,800 total. Who pays doc stamps in Tampa, FL?

In Florida, documentary stamp tax on the deed (doc stamps) is paid by the seller at $0.70 per $100 of the sale price. On a $700,000 sale, the seller pays $4,900 in doc stamps. The buyer pays a separate intangible tax on their mortgage note — $0.002 per $1 of loan amount. On a $560,000 mortgage (80% of a $700K purchase), that's $1,120.

Is Hillsborough County different from Pinellas County for closing costs?

Yes — title insurance customs differ across Florida counties, and Hillsborough and Pinellas operate differently. In Hillsborough County (Tampa), the buyer typically pays for both title policies. In Pinellas County (St. Pete, Clearwater), the seller more often pays for the owner's title policy. This difference can add or subtract $2,000–$5,000 from your closing cost estimate depending on which county you're buying in.

Can a buyer negotiate who pays title insurance in Hillsborough County?

Yes. The Florida Realtors/Florida Bar contracts allow the parties to negotiate who pays for title insurance. In a buyer's market, it's reasonable to ask the seller to contribute to or cover title costs as a seller concession. In a competitive market, asking for title concessions can hurt your offer's competitiveness. Your agent will know the current conditions and when this ask makes sense.
