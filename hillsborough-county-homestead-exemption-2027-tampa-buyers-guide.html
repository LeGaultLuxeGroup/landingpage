## How does Florida's new $150,000 homestead exemption affect Tampa buyers in 2027? Florida voters approved Amendment 3 on November 4, 2026, raising the homestead exemption from $50,000 to $150,000 effective January 1, 2027. Tampa buyers who close before December 31, 2026 and establish the property as their primary residence can file for the new exemption with the Hillsborough County Property Appraiser by March 1, 2027. The result: $1,500–$2,500 in additional annual property tax savings compared to what buyers under the old exemption would have seen — and that number compounds every year the Save Our Homes cap holds your assessment down. By Amanda LeGault | November 2026 --- If you're under contract on a Tampa home right now, there's a number on the table that most buyers haven't had time to calculate yet. Florida voters approved Amendment 3 six days ago. Starting January 1, 2027, the homestead exemption jumps from $50,000 to $150,000. That's $100,000 more of your home's assessed value sheltered from property taxes — every single year you own it as your primary residence. Here's the catch that no one is talking about: you need to close before January 1, 2027 to claim it for the 2027 tax year. That's 52 days from today. If you close on January 2nd, your first shot at the new exemption is 2028. This isn't a marketing hook. It's how Florida property tax law works, and right now it's one of the most consequential deadlines in the Tampa market. --- ## What the New Homestead Exemption Actually Means for Your Property Taxes Florida's homestead exemption has been $50,000 since 1994. For more than three decades, that number sat unchanged while Tampa home values climbed from the $100s to well into the $500s and $600s in South Tampa, New Tampa, and Westchase. Amendment 3 triples that number. Starting with the 2027 tax year, the first $150,000 of your home's assessed value is shielded from most property taxes. Here's what that looks like in real dollars. Take a $500,000 home in the City of Tampa. Combined millage rates in Tampa run roughly 20–26 mills depending on your municipality and service district. Using a conservative 22 mills as a blended rate: **Under the old $50,000 exemption:** - Taxable assessed value: $450,000 - Annual tax (at 22 mills): approximately $9,900 **Under the new $150,000 exemption:** - Taxable assessed value: $350,000 - Annual tax (at 22 mills): approximately $7,700 That's a $2,200/year difference. Over five years, you're keeping an extra $11,000 in your pocket. For a $400,000 home in unincorporated Hillsborough County, where combined millage rates run closer to 18–19 mills, the annual difference is around $1,700–$1,900. Your actual savings depend on your home's assessed value and your specific tax district. The Hillsborough County Property Appraiser's website has a tax estimator you can use to run your own numbers. --- **The December 31 Deadline Is Real** Florida's homestead exemption is attached to January 1 ownership. The Property Appraiser assesses values as of January 1 each year. To qualify for the 2027 homestead exemption — including the new $150,000 amount — you must own the property and have established it as your primary residence as of January 1, 2027. That means your deed needs to be recorded before midnight on December 31, 2026. If you're in contract now with a closing scheduled for mid-to-late December, you're in position. If you're still searching, you're working against the clock — in Tampa's current market, the typical timeline from contract to close is 30–45 days. Buyers who close January 1 or later will get homestead starting with the 2028 tax year. That's not the end of the world, but it means missing the first year of the new $150,000 exemption and one more year of your Save Our Homes cap accruing. --- ## How to File Your Homestead Exemption with Hillsborough County Once you've closed, filing homestead is straightforward. The Hillsborough County Property Appraiser handles all exemption applications. Here's the process: **Step 1: File online or in person by March 1, 2027.** The Property Appraiser's office accepts online applications through their website. You can also file in person at their downtown Tampa office (601 E Kennedy Blvd) or at their Brandon satellite office. **Step 2: What you'll need.** - Florida driver's license or state ID with your new address - Vehicle registration with your new address (or proof you've updated it) - Your Social Security number - The property's parcel ID (on your closing documents) - Proof of permanent residency if you're a permanent resident alien **Step 3: Update your license and vehicle registration first.** Florida's homestead law requires that you've declared Florida as your permanent state of domicile. If you're moving from out of state, update your driver's license and vehicle registration to your Tampa address before you file. The Property Appraiser verifies residency through these documents. The deadline is March 1, 2027. File early — the office gets busy in February, and there's no reason to cut it close on a tax benefit worth thousands of dollars a year. --- ## The Second Benefit You Get Automatically: Save Our Homes The homestead exemption is just the beginning. When you file homestead, you also lock in Florida's Save Our Homes assessment cap. Here's how it works: once you have homestead, the assessed value of your home can only increase by 3% or the rate of inflation — whichever is lower — each year. No matter how fast Tampa's market moves. This is enormously valuable over time. Tampa home values jumped significantly in recent years. Without the Save Our Homes cap, your assessed value — and your property tax bill — rises with the market. With it, you're insulated. The cap kicks in starting the year you establish homestead. So if you close in December 2026 and file for 2027 homestead, your 2027 assessed value becomes the baseline, and growth from there is capped. A buyer who waits until 2028 misses an entire year of cap protection. If you're buying a Tampa home as your primary residence and you're not planning to file homestead the same year you close, you're leaving money on the table. --- ## If You Already Own a Tampa Home: Portability Already have homestead somewhere in Florida? You may be able to bring your accumulated Save Our Homes benefit with you. Florida portability lets you transfer up to $500,000 of your previous home's difference between assessed value and market value to your new home. That means your new Tampa property could start with a significantly lower assessed value from day one — compounding your tax savings year over year. You apply for portability at the same time you file your new homestead application. The transfer requires a completed DR-501T form. If your previous Florida homestead sold in the last three years, you likely qualify. Some jurisdictions offer a full exemption for qualifying low-income seniors. **Veterans:** Service-connected disability of 10% or more qualifies for an additional exemption. 100% disabled veterans may be fully exempt from property taxes. Surviving spouses of honorably discharged veterans receive a portion of this benefit. **First responders and military:** Surviving spouses of first responders killed in the line of duty are fully exempt. Active-duty military may qualify for additional protections. If any of these apply to you, make sure the Property Appraiser knows at the time you file. These aren't automatic — you have to apply. --- ## What This Means for Your Buying Decision Right Now For buyers who've been debating whether to close before year-end or push into early 2027, Amendment 3 adds real financial urgency to the decision.
