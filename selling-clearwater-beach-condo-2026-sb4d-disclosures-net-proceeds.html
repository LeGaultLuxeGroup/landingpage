Selling a Clearwater Beach Condo in 2026: Pricing, SB 4D Disclosures, and What You'll Actually Net

How do you sell a Clearwater Beach condo in 2026's buyer's market?

Price to the current comps, not last year's, and hand buyers a complete disclosure package on day one: the milestone inspection summary, the SIRS, the budget, and any special assessments, in writing. Clearwater Beach has more than 8 months of condo and home supply and about 29% of Pinellas listings have taken a price cut, so sellers who disclose early and offer targeted concessions sell faster and net more than sellers who wait for a buyer to find the problems.

By Amanda LeGault | November 27, 2026

If you own a condo on Clearwater Beach or Sand Key, you've probably noticed the listings around you aren't moving like they did in 2022.

That doesn't mean you can't sell. It means the sellers who win right now are doing three things differently: they price to today's market, they get ahead of the SB 4D questions, and they know their real net before they list.

Here's how I walk my Clearwater Beach sellers through it.

What the Clearwater Beach market looks like right now

The numbers send mixed signals, and that's exactly why pricing is tricky.

  • Prices have held up. One market tracker using MLS data as of November 16, 2026 puts the Clearwater Beach median sale price around $740,000, up roughly 11% year over year.
  • But supply is heavy. The same tracker shows about 220 homes and condos on the market and more than 8 months of supply. Anything over 6 months is generally a buyer's market.
  • Buyers are taking their time. Homes are averaging about 76 days to go under contract.
  • Price cuts are common. Roughly 29% of Pinellas County listings have taken a reduction.

The takeaway: well-priced, well-documented units in solid buildings are still selling at strong numbers. Overpriced units in buildings with open questions are sitting, and every month they sit, buyers assume something's wrong.

The SB 4D question every buyer will ask

Since Florida's condo safety laws took effect, the first question a serious Clearwater Beach buyer asks isn't about the view. It's "What's going on with the building?"

Florida now requires a seller of a resale condo to provide the buyer with the association's governing documents, the most recent year-end financial statement, and the FAQ sheet, along with the building's milestone inspection summary and structural integrity reserve study (SIRS) where they apply. The buyer has a short window after receiving those documents to cancel.

That cancellation right is why late disclosure kills deals. If your buyer learns about a $60,000 special assessment on day 2 of their review period, they walk, and you've lost two to three weeks of market time.

How to get ahead of it

  1. Order the resale package before you list. Request the declaration, bylaws, rules, budget, year-end financials, FAQ sheet, milestone inspection summary, and SIRS from your association manager now.
  2. Get the assessment picture in writing. Ask the manager for every special assessment that's been levied, the payment schedule, and anything the board is discussing. Board minutes from the last 12 months tell you a lot.
  3. Order an early estoppel estimate. The estoppel certificate confirms what's owed to the association at closing. Florida caps the fee, but you don't want surprises in week 4.
  4. Check lender eligibility. Buildings with unfunded critical repairs, low reserves, or active litigation can be ineligible for Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac financing. If your building is one of them, your buyer pool shrinks to cash and portfolio lenders, and your pricing should reflect that.
  5. Write a one-page building summary. Inspection status, reserve funding, completed repairs, and assessments, in plain English. Buyers and their agents love it, and it signals you have nothing to hide.

Pricing, concessions, and who pays the assessment

Price to the building, not the beach

Two Gulf-view two-bedrooms on Mandalay Avenue can be $150,000 apart because of what's happening in their buildings. When I price a Clearwater Beach condo, I'm comparing:

  • Recent closed sales in your building, not just nearby
  • Reserve funding and remaining assessment balances
  • Insurance costs for the master policy and an HO-6 (see our Clearwater Beach condo insurance guide)
  • Rental rules, which matter a lot to investor buyers
  • Flood zone, view, floor, and renovation level

Pricing right from day one beats chasing the market down. In a market with 8 months of supply, your first two weeks bring your best traffic. A listing that needs two price cuts usually sells for less than one that was priced correctly at the start.

Use concessions strategically

Instead of a large price cut, consider a targeted concession that solves the buyer's actual problem:

  • Paying off or crediting a special assessment at closing. Buyers often value this more than an equal price reduction.
  • A mortgage rate buydown credit, which can lower the buyer's monthly payment more than the same dollars off the price.
  • An HOA dues or insurance credit for the first year.

Who pays a special assessment is negotiable in your contract. The standard Florida contract addresses assessments imposed before closing, but you and the buyer can agree to any split. Decide your position before you list, not when the offer arrives.

What you'll actually net selling a Clearwater Beach condo

Here's a sample on a $750,000 Clearwater Beach condo. Your numbers will differ.

  • Florida doc stamps on the deed: $0.70 per $100, or $5,250. The seller customarily pays this in Florida.
  • Real estate commissions: negotiable. At 5%–6% total, that's $37,500–$45,000.
  • Title and settlement fees: a few thousand dollars, depending on who pays the owner's title policy in your contract.
  • Estoppel certificate and association fees: usually a few hundred dollars.
  • Prorated property taxes and HOA dues through the closing date.
  • Special assessment balance or credit: anything you agree to pay at closing.
  • Your mortgage payoff.

In most Clearwater Beach sales, total selling costs land around 7%–9% of the sale price before your mortgage payoff and any assessment credits. For a deeper breakdown of Pinellas seller costs, see our St. Petersburg seller closing costs guide. The same doc stamp rate and closing process apply in Clearwater.

If you've owned the unit as a rental or second home, talk to your CPA about capital gains and depreciation recapture before you list. If it's your homestead, the federal home-sale exclusion may cover much of your gain.

Your actual net depends on your building, your assessment situation, and your price. That's what a local market analysis is for.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is it a good time to sell a Clearwater Beach condo?

It depends on your building and your goals. Clearwater Beach prices have held up, with a median around $740,000 in mid-November 2026, but more than 8 months of supply means buyers are selective. Well-priced units with clean disclosures are selling, and units in buildings with open SB 4D questions are taking longer.

Do I have to disclose a special assessment when I sell my Clearwater condo?

Yes. Florida requires condo sellers to provide the association's governing documents, financial information, and building safety reports, and any levied assessment shows up in the estoppel certificate. Disclose assessments early and in writing, because the buyer can cancel during their document review period.

Who pays the special assessment when a Florida condo sells?

It's negotiable. The standard Florida contract addresses assessments imposed before closing, but buyer and seller can agree to any split. Many Clearwater Beach sellers offer to pay the remaining balance or give a credit at closing to keep the deal together.

How much does it cost to sell a condo on Clearwater Beach?

Most sellers spend about 7%–9% of the sale price, including Florida doc stamps of $0.70 per $100, negotiated commissions, title and settlement fees, the estoppel certificate, and prorations. On a $750,000 condo, doc stamps alone are $5,250.

Can a buyer get a mortgage on a Clearwater Beach condo with SB 4D issues?

Sometimes not. Buildings with unfunded critical repairs, low reserves, or active litigation may be ineligible for Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac loans, which limits buyers to cash or portfolio lenders. Check your building's lender eligibility before you price your unit.

The bottom line

In 2026, Clearwater Beach condos sell when the price matches the building and the buyer gets the full story up front. Order your documents early, decide your concession strategy before you list, and know your net before you sign a listing agreement.
