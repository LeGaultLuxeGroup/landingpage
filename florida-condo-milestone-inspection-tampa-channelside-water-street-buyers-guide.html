Florida Condo Milestone Inspections in Tampa, FL: What Water Street, Channelside, and Downtown Buyers Must Know Before Closing

What is Florida's SB 4D milestone inspection law, and does it affect Tampa condo buyers?

Florida's SB 4D law requires condominium associations in buildings three stories or taller to complete milestone structural inspections when their building turns 30 years old — or 25 years if within three miles of the coastline. A passing Phase 1 inspection clears the building; a failing Phase 1 triggers a Phase 2 engineering review that can reveal repair requirements and reserve shortfalls. In Tampa, this law affects resale buyers in Channelside, downtown, South Tampa, and Hyde Park — and lenders are now requiring written confirmation of milestone inspection status before approving condo loans. Buying a Tampa condo without verifying SB 4D compliance is one of the fastest ways to end up with a lender hold, a failed closing, or a special assessment surprise six months later.

By Amanda LeGault, LeGault Luxe Group at Keller Williams  |  September 29, 2026

If you're shopping for a condo in Tampa right now — downtown, Channelside, Hyde Park, or anywhere other than brand-new Water Street construction — there's a Florida law you need to understand before you write an offer.

Senate Bill 4D passed in 2022 as Florida's response to the Champlain Towers collapse in Surfside. It created mandatory structural milestone inspections and reserve funding requirements for condominium associations. By now, the first wave of deadlines has passed — meaning every Tampa condo building that triggered the requirement either completed its inspection or it didn't. If it didn't, your transaction could be affected.

This isn't hypothetical. Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac have both issued guidance requiring lenders to verify milestone inspection status before approving financing on affected condominiums. If a building failed to comply, it can become unlendable — meaning no conventional financing, period.

Here's what you need to know before you fall in love with a Tampa condo.

Which Tampa condo buildings are in scope for SB 4D?

The law applies to residential condominium buildings that are three stories or taller and have reached 30 years of age (or 25 years for buildings within three miles of the coastline). The building age is measured from the Certificate of Occupancy date.

In Tampa, that creates a clear dividing line.

Not yet in scope — new construction (generally exempt for now):
Water Street Tampa's new residential towers — including those opened from 2020–2024 — are roughly 20–25 years away from the 30-year milestone inspection trigger. If you're buying new construction in Water Street or Channel District, SB 4D isn't your immediate concern. That said, the SIRS (Structural Integrity Reserve Study) requirement applies to all condominiums, new and old — and you should still verify that the association has fully funded its reserves from the start.

In scope now — older downtown and Channelside-area buildings:
Buildings constructed in the mid-1990s through the early 2000s are hitting or approaching the 30-year window. In the Tampa condo market, this includes a significant portion of the Channelside, downtown, South Tampa, and Hyde Park resale inventory. Buildings constructed near or along the Hillsborough River and Tampa Bay may qualify for the 25-year coastal threshold.

What to ask before making an offer on any resale Tampa condo:

  1. Has the association completed its Phase 1 milestone inspection?
  2. If Phase 1 found concerns, was a Phase 2 inspection ordered — and what were the findings?
  3. Has the association completed its SIRS (Structural Integrity Reserve Study)?
  4. What percentage of SIRS-required reserves is currently funded?
  5. Are any special assessments pending or under discussion?

Under Florida's condo disclosure rules, you have the right to this documentation. Associations are required to provide it. If an association refuses, delays, or claims they don't have it — that's a red flag, not a paperwork inconvenience.

What happens when a building fails Phase 1 — and why it matters to your mortgage

Phase 1 is a visual structural assessment by a licensed architect or engineer. Most buildings pass Phase 1. But when Phase 1 identifies structural concerns — concrete deterioration, waterproofing failures, balcony spalling — a Phase 2 inspection is required. Phase 2 is a more invasive engineering review that determines the scope and cost of needed repairs.

Here's where it gets expensive for buyers: if a building has underfunded reserves and a Phase 2 inspection reveals repair requirements, the association has limited options. They can draw from reserves (if funded), issue a special assessment, or obtain a loan. An underfunded SIRS combined with a problematic Phase 2 report is a combination that can lead to five- or six-figure special assessments within a year or two of purchase.

The Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac guidelines add another layer. Both agencies now require lenders to confirm — before loan approval — that the building has completed its required milestone inspection, is not under a significant deferred maintenance or repair order, and the association is not subject to a regulatory action. A building that failed to complete its milestone inspection by its deadline may be flagged as a non-warrantable condo, meaning conventional financing terms change significantly or become unavailable.

I've had buyers in Tampa come within days of closing before discovering their lender couldn't approve the loan because the association hadn't filed its Phase 1 report. That's a situation that's completely avoidable if you ask the right questions during the inspection period.

Before removing your inspection contingency on any Tampa condo purchase, confirm in writing:

  • The Phase 1 milestone inspection report (or confirmation the building is not yet in scope)
  • If Phase 2 was triggered, the Phase 2 report and any required remediation plan
  • The most recent SIRS and the association's current reserve funding percentage
  • Minutes from the last 12 months of board meetings — specifically for any discussion of structural repairs, special assessments, or reserve underfunding

Your real estate attorney or agent should help you request these through the formal condo disclosure process. Florida law gives you the right to them, but you have to ask.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does SB 4D apply to all Tampa condos, or just old ones?

The milestone inspection requirement under SB 4D applies to residential condo buildings that are three or more stories tall and at least 30 years old — or 25 years old if the building is within three miles of the coastline. Brand-new buildings in Water Street Tampa, Channel District, and similar developments are exempt for now. However, the SIRS (Structural Integrity Reserve Study) requirement applies to all Florida condominium associations, regardless of building age, and must have been completed by December 31, 2024. Buyers should verify SIRS compliance for any Tampa condo, new or old.

Can I still get a mortgage on a Tampa condo if the building hasn't completed its milestone inspection?

It depends on the lender and the building's status. Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac — which back the majority of conventional mortgages — now require lenders to verify milestone inspection compliance for applicable buildings. A building that missed its inspection deadline or has outstanding Phase 2 findings may be designated as a non-warrantable condo, which can limit your financing options and raise your interest rate. FHA and VA loans have their own condo project approval requirements. Always confirm the building's condo project status with your lender before going under contract.

How do I find out if a Tampa condo building has completed its milestone inspection?

Ask the association directly during the Florida condo disclosure period — you're legally entitled to the documentation. The City of Tampa also maintains a condo recertification program; for buildings subject to Chapter 553 of the Florida Building Code, records may be available through the city's construction services portal at tampa.gov. Your real estate agent and title company can help you request the full disclosure package from the association within the statutory period.

What is a SIRS and how does it affect my decision to buy a Tampa condo?

A Structural Integrity Reserve Study (SIRS) is an engineering assessment that identifies the condition of a building's major components — roof, exterior, parking structure, plumbing, HVAC, and similar systems — and calculates the cost to repair or replace each one over time. The association uses the SIRS to set reserve funding levels. If an association's reserves are significantly underfunded relative to what the SIRS recommends, the gap may eventually be filled through a special assessment to unit owners — meaning you as the buyer could face a lump-sum charge after closing. Always ask for the most recent SIRS and the current reserve funding percentage before making an offer.

Are Water Street Tampa condos subject to the SB 4D inspection law?

Not yet. Water Street Tampa's residential towers were completed from 2020–2024, putting them roughly 20–25 years away from the standard 30-year milestone inspection trigger. However, since some Water Street buildings are near Tampa Bay, they may qualify for the 25-year coastal threshold — bringing inspections closer to the mid-2040s. For new Water Street condo buyers, the more relevant questions are SIRS compliance and reserve funding, since those requirements apply from the moment an association is formed.

The SB 4D law is genuinely good for buyers — it creates transparency about building structural health that didn't exist before. But it only helps you if you know what to ask. In Tampa's active condo market, especially in Channelside and downtown, the difference between a smooth closing and a blown deal often comes down to whether someone pulled the milestone inspection report before week three.
