Buying Pre-Construction in Sarasota? What Every Buyer Must Know Before Signing

What should I know before buying a pre-construction condo in Sarasota?

Florida law requires developers to hold pre-construction deposits in escrow until the condo is complete — but your deposit protections only kick in if you verify the escrow arrangement before you sign. You also have a 15-day window to rescind a Florida pre-construction condo contract for any reason. Before signing with any Sarasota developer, confirm the escrow structure, understand what "subject to financing" actually covers in a developer contract, and research the developer's track record on completed projects.

What the sales center doesn't hand you is a plain-language explanation of what you're actually signing.

A pre-construction contract is not a standard real estate purchase agreement. It's a developer document — written by the developer's attorneys, for the developer's benefit. That doesn't make it predatory. But it does mean there are things you need to check before your signature goes on page one, and several places where buyers assume protections exist that don't.

Here's what I walk through with every client who's considering a pre-construction purchase in Sarasota.

Your Deposit Is Protected — But Only If You Verify the Arrangement

Florida law requires developers to hold buyer deposits in escrow until the condominium is complete and you receive your deed. Specifically, Florida Statutes §718.202 sets this up for condo pre-construction — the developer must either maintain your deposit in a proper escrow account or substitute a surety bond or irrevocable letter of credit in the same amount.

This is meaningful protection. It means that if the developer runs out of money mid-construction and the project fails, your deposit comes back to you.

The catch: you need to confirm in writing which arrangement applies to your contract, and you need to know who holds the escrow. "Your deposit is protected" is not the same as "we have confirmed escrow with [specific title company]." Get the specifics before you sign — not after.

Some buyers also discover that deposits are released to the developer in phases rather than held in full escrow through closing. Florida law permits this under certain conditions. Review your contract language carefully, or have your attorney review it, to understand exactly when your money leaves escrow.

You Have 15 Days to Change Your Mind — and Then You Don't

Florida law gives pre-construction condo buyers a 15-day rescission window. Starting from the day you sign the contract — or the day you receive the condo documents (disclosure package), whichever is later — you have 15 calendar days to cancel for any reason and receive a full refund of your deposit.

This is your clearest exit. Use it. Within those 15 days, have an attorney review the contract, go through the condo documents, check the developer's track record, and make sure you understand the full cost picture.

After day 15, your deposit is typically non-refundable unless the developer is in breach of contract. "I changed my mind" doesn't entitle you to a refund. "I found a better deal elsewhere" doesn't either. The rescission window is the moment of maximum flexibility — treat it that way.

What "Subject to Financing" Means in a Developer Contract

In a standard resale purchase in Sarasota, a financing contingency protects you if your mortgage falls through — you can back out and get your deposit returned. Developer contracts often look similar but aren't.

Many pre-construction contracts include financing contingency language, but with important limitations. Some require you to obtain financing approval within a specific (often very short) window. Others specify that approval must be for a certain loan amount. A few have no meaningful financing protection at all, or tie the contingency to conditions the developer controls.

More importantly: you're getting a mortgage for a condo that doesn't exist yet. Your lender can approve you today based on current rates, your current income, and a market appraisal for a similar completed unit — and then rates rise, your income situation changes, or the appraisal comes in low at closing (two or three years from now) and the loan falls short. The developer may argue you're still obligated to close or forfeit your deposit.

Work with a lender who has experience with pre-construction financing before you sign, and understand exactly what "subject to financing" means in your specific contract — not in your assumptions about it.

The Deposit Structure and What You're Committing To

Pre-construction deposits in Sarasota luxury towers typically run 10–20% of the purchase price, sometimes higher for top-floor or penthouse units. That money is often paid in phases:

  • An initial deposit at signing (often 5–10%)
  • Additional installments tied to construction milestones (groundbreaking, topping out, etc.)
  • The balance at closing when the certificate of occupancy is issued

On a $1.5 million Sarasota condo, you might have $150,000–$300,000 deployed before you ever set foot in the finished unit. Confirm how each tranche is handled in escrow, what triggers each payment, and what happens to those funds if construction is delayed or the project is restructured.

Also important: pre-construction prices in Sarasota are typically fixed at contract — which means you're betting on appreciation, and you're exposed if the market softens between contract and close. This isn't a reason not to buy pre-construction; it's a reason to understand the risk you're taking on.

Evaluating the Developer Before You Sign

The developer's track record is not a detail you research after you sign. It's one of the first things you verify.

Look for: completed projects (not just announced ones), how those projects delivered relative to their timelines and promised specifications, any litigation history on prior developments, and whether the entity signing your contract has a clear connection to the development entity that actually completes projects.

A few things to check specifically for Sarasota pre-construction:

  • Is the development fully permitted, or are permits still pending?
  • What percentage of units must be pre-sold before construction begins? (This tells you how close to groundbreaking they actually are.)
  • What is the projected delivery timeline, and does the contract allow the developer to extend it?
  • Has construction financing been secured, or is the developer pre-selling to fund construction?

None of these questions are hostile to the developer. Any reputable development team answers them readily. If you're getting vague answers about construction financing or permitting status, that's information too.

Costs That Buyers Don't Always See Coming

The purchase price is what you sign for. The total cost of a pre-construction condo in Sarasota includes a few other items worth modeling in advance.

Closing costs still apply — and in Sarasota County, the buyer typically pays for title insurance, which is the opposite of how it works in many other Florida counties. On a $1.5M purchase, that's a meaningful number. Factor it in.

Developer contracts sometimes include "closing cost caps" that limit what the developer can charge at closing — but they don't always cap everything. Review what you're responsible for beyond the purchase price.

Monthly carrying costs matter too: HOA fees in newly built luxury towers in downtown Sarasota can run $1,000–$3,000+/month depending on amenities, and those numbers are estimates until the building is operating. Budgets sometimes change. Also worth confirming: CDD fees (Community Development Districts) are not common in downtown Sarasota towers but are worth verifying, especially for any pre-construction development in planned communities outside the urban core.

The One Thing the Sales Center Won't Tell You

The developer's on-site sales team is not your representative. They're the developer's representative. They're good at their jobs, they know the product well, and they're not adversarial — but they work for the other side of the table.

A buyer's agent costs you nothing on a pre-construction purchase. In Florida, the developer pays the buyer's agent commission on pre-construction sales — it's built into the developer's marketing budget, not added to your purchase price. Walking into a sales center without representation doesn't save you money. It just means you're negotiating against a professional team with no professional on your side.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is my pre-construction deposit protected in Florida?

Yes — Florida law (F.S. 718.202) requires developers to hold buyer deposits in an escrow account until the condominium is complete and title transfers, or the developer can substitute a surety bond or irrevocable letter of credit in the same amount. Always confirm in writing which arrangement applies to your contract. If the project fails and the developer cannot deliver, your escrowed deposit must be returned.

Can I back out of a pre-construction condo contract in Sarasota?

Yes — Florida law gives you a 15-day rescission window after you sign (or after you receive the condo documents, whichever is later). Within that window, you can cancel for any reason and get your deposit back. After 15 days, deposits are typically non-refundable unless the developer is in breach of contract.

What happens if a Sarasota pre-construction development is never completed?

If your deposit was properly escrowed under Florida law, it must be returned to you if the developer cannot complete the project and deliver your unit. The developer's failure to deliver constitutes a breach, triggering the return of escrowed funds. This is why confirming the escrow arrangement before signing — not after — is non-negotiable.

Do pre-construction condos in downtown Sarasota have CDD fees?

CDD (Community Development District) fees are more common in master-planned communities like Lakewood Ranch than in urban downtown Sarasota towers. Most downtown Sarasota pre-construction condos do not carry CDD fees, but review the developer's condo documents and the property's Sarasota County records to confirm before signing.

Should I use a buyer's agent when buying pre-construction in Sarasota?

Yes — and it costs you nothing. In Florida, the developer pays the buyer's agent commission on pre-construction sales. The developer's on-site sales team represents the developer's interests, not yours. A buyer's agent reviews the contract, flags risks, negotiates upgrades and closing cost contributions, and represents you if issues arise during construction.
