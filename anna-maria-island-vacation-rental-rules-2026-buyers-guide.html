Anna Maria Island Vacation Rental Rules 2026: What Every Buyer Needs to Know Before Closing

Can you short-term rent a home on Anna Maria Island?

Yes — all three island cities (City of Anna Maria, Holmes Beach, and Bradenton Beach) allow short-term vacation rentals in 2026. But each city operates under its own permit requirements, occupancy limits, and in Holmes Beach, zoning-based minimum stay rules that can require a 30-day minimum in certain areas. Buyers also need to check HOA and condo association documents, which can impose stricter restrictions — including outright rental bans — regardless of what the city allows. If rental income is part of your purchase calculus, you need to verify four things before making an offer: the city, the zoning, the HOA documents, and the current registration requirements.

Here's the question I hear from almost every buyer who calls me about Anna Maria Island: "Can I rent this out on Airbnb?"

It's the right question. At the $1 million to $5 million price point AMI commands, rental income isn't just a nice-to-have — it's often what makes the numbers work. A well-positioned three-bedroom on the island can generate $80,000 to $150,000 or more in annual gross rental income. For a lot of buyers, that's the difference between a stretch purchase and a smart one.

But the answer isn't a simple yes or no. It's: it depends on which city the property sits in, what zone it's in, and what the HOA says.

Get those three things wrong and you could close on a $2 million island home expecting weekly vacation rentals — and discover you're looking at a 30-day minimum or a condo board that bans short-term rentals entirely. I've seen it happen. Here's what you need to know so it doesn't happen to you.

The Island Has Three Cities — Each With Its Own Rules

Anna Maria Island sits in Manatee County, but it's divided into three separate incorporated cities: the City of Anna Maria (northernmost), Holmes Beach (the middle and largest), and Bradenton Beach (southernmost). Each city has its own vacation rental ordinance, its own permit process, and its own enforcement approach.

Manatee County itself — the unincorporated areas — currently has no county-level short-term rental ordinance. So if a property sits inside city limits (which most island properties do), city rules govern. If it sits in unincorporated Manatee County, the state license is the primary hurdle, though tax registration still applies.

All three island cities are grandfathered under Florida's 2011 vacation rental preemption law, which generally prevents newer local ordinances from banning rentals. Because these cities had their own vacation rental regulations before that law took effect, they retain the authority to regulate — and enforce — their own rules. That's why the island has stayed a viable vacation rental market while some Florida communities have faced more restrictions.

City of Anna Maria

The northernmost and quietest city on the island, City of Anna Maria is roughly 95% single-family homes — no large condo complexes. Most properties allow nightly rentals, and the registration process, while mandatory, is relatively straightforward once you have a Florida DBPR Transient Public Lodging Establishment (TPLE) license in hand.

You'll need to register with the city annually, pay the applicable fees, and pass an annual inspection that checks code compliance, safety equipment, and proper posting of occupancy limits and emergency contacts. Occupancy is capped at 8 — calculated as two guests per qualifying bedroom, plus two additional — so a three-bedroom home would max out at eight guests.

Anna Maria properties tend to run about 5% higher in price than comparable Holmes Beach properties. You're paying for the quieter, more residential feel — and buyers who want that peace alongside vacation rental income tend to gravitate here.

Holmes Beach

Holmes Beach is the largest of the three cities and home to the highest concentration of vacation rentals. It's also the city with the most complexity for buyers — because minimum stay requirements are set by zoning district, not uniformly across the city.

Some Holmes Beach zones allow weekly (7-day minimum) rentals. Others require a 30-day minimum. The difference between the zones can literally be the same street, so you can't assume based on neighborhood — you need to look up the specific parcel's zoning classification on the Holmes Beach zoning map or confirm it with the city's planning department.

The vacation rental certificate process here is the most involved of the three cities: you'll need a certificate (valid for two years, then renewed), a code enforcement inspection of the property before you can list it for rent, and more supporting documentation than Anna Maria requires. Plan for this process before you're under contract — it takes time.

Occupancy in Holmes Beach is 6 guests or two per bedroom, whichever is greater. For most two- and three-bedroom homes, that's 6.

Bradenton Beach

The southernmost and narrowest part of the island, Bradenton Beach is the most walkable stretch — close to Bridge Street restaurants, shops, and the intercoastal waterway. It's about 80% condos and 20% single-family. Nightly rentals are allowed city-wide, with no published minimum stay.

The registration requirements include the DBPR TPLE license, city registration, and two things specific to Bradenton Beach: an exterior identification label that must be posted on the property whenever it's being rented, and a floor plan posted inside the unit. Occupancy goes up to 12, calculated as two per bedroom plus two, or one per 100 square feet of climate-controlled space.

Prices in Bradenton Beach run about 5% lower than Holmes Beach, which can make the numbers work well for investment-focused buyers.

The Variable That Overrides Everything: Your HOA

This is where I see buyers get burned most often.

Even if the city allows nightly rentals, a homeowners association or condo association can impose stricter rules. That means a Bradenton Beach condo might sit in a city that allows nightly rentals — but the condo association's governing documents require a 90-day minimum. Or prohibit rentals entirely for the first year of ownership. Or cap the number of rental weeks per year.

These restrictions appear in the condo declaration, the CC&Rs (covenants, conditions, and restrictions), and the association's bylaws. They are legally binding and can be enforced against you as the new owner, even if the previous owner had no rental restrictions at all.

Request these documents during due diligence — not after closing. Florida law requires sellers to provide condominium documents within 3 business days of a signed contract, and buyers have 3 business days after receipt to cancel for any reason. Use that window to review the rental restrictions carefully. Your attorney or title company can help you spot the relevant provisions.

If a vacation rental income stream is central to your decision, make it a condition of your offer to have the governing documents in hand before you waive your inspection contingency. I build this into the process for every buyer I represent on the island.

The Four Things to Verify Before You Make an Offer

When a buyer asks me about an AMI investment property, here's the checklist I walk them through:

  1. Which city is it in? City of Anna Maria, Holmes Beach, or Bradenton Beach each have different rules. If the property is in unincorporated Manatee County, the county has no STR ordinance — confirm with your agent.
  2. What zone is it in? This matters most in Holmes Beach. A weekly-minimum zone and a monthly-minimum zone can sit across the street from each other. Look up the parcel on Holmes Beach's official zoning map or call the city directly.
  3. What do the HOA or condo docs say? Request them before making an offer if possible, or as an early due diligence item. Know the minimum stay requirement and any other rental restrictions before you're committed.
  4. What licenses does the current owner have — and are they transferable? Florida DBPR TPLE licenses are registered to the owner, not the property. You'll need to apply for your own after closing. Some city registrations and certificates also require a new application from the new owner. Factor in the time and cost to get operational.

Your specific numbers depend on the property's location, the zoning, what the HOA allows, and your rental strategy — that's exactly the kind of analysis I walk my clients through before we write an offer.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can you do short-term rentals on Anna Maria Island?

Yes — all three island cities (City of Anna Maria, Holmes Beach, and Bradenton Beach) allow short-term vacation rentals. However, each city has its own permit requirements, minimum stay rules, occupancy limits, and registration process. Holmes Beach adds a layer of zoning-based minimums: some zones require a 30-day minimum stay, while others allow weekly rentals. Always confirm which city a property sits in and what that city's current rules are before making an offer.

What permits are required to rent out an Anna Maria Island home?

At minimum, you'll need a Florida Department of Business and Professional Regulation (DBPR) Transient Public Lodging Establishment (TPLE) license for any property rented for fewer than 30 days. Beyond that, each city requires its own registration: the City of Anna Maria requires annual registration and inspection; Holmes Beach requires a vacation rental certificate (renewed every two years) and a code enforcement inspection before the first rental; Bradenton Beach requires the DBPR license, city registration, and an exterior identification label displayed whenever the property is being rented.

Can an HOA or condo association restrict rentals on Anna Maria Island?

Yes — and this catches buyers off guard more than anything else. Even if the city allows nightly rentals, a homeowners association or condo association can impose stricter rules, including 30-day or 90-day minimum stays, outright rental bans, or caps on the number of rentals per year. These restrictions appear in the condo documents or HOA CC&Rs. You must review these before making an offer — not after. Your agent can request the governing documents during due diligence.

Is Manatee County (unincorporated) subject to the same vacation rental rules as the island cities?

No — Manatee County currently has no county-level short-term rental ordinance for unincorporated areas. If a property is in unincorporated Manatee County rather than within one of the three island city limits, the state DBPR TPLE license is the primary requirement, though county and state tax registration still apply. Properties just off the island in unincorporated Manatee County may have fewer local hurdles, though HOA restrictions can still apply.

What occupancy limits apply to Anna Maria Island vacation rentals?

Occupancy limits differ by city. The City of Anna Maria caps most properties at 8 guests. Bradenton Beach allows up to 12. Holmes Beach limits occupancy to 6 guests or two per bedroom, whichever is greater. These limits are enforced — exceeding them can result in code enforcement, fines, and loss of your rental license.
