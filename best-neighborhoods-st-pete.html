By Amanda LeGault · LeGault Luxe Group · Keller Williams on the Water · Last updated July 2026 St. Petersburg has more than 30 distinct neighborhoods, ranging from waterfront estates on Snell Isle to arts-district bungalows in Historic Kenwood. I've helped buyers relocate here from Chicago, New York, and the Bay Area — and the question I get most often isn't "should I move to St. Pete?" It's "which neighborhood is actually right for me?" This guide answers that. Neighborhoods Covered Old Northeast — historic, walkable, $600K–$900K Snell Isle — luxury waterfront estates, $1M–$5M+ Shore Acres — canal homes, boating access, $600K–$1.5M Downtown St. Pete — urban, walkable, condos $350K–$1.2M Historic Kenwood — arts district, bungalows, $350K–$550K Tierra Verde — barrier island, luxury, $800K–$3M+ Coffee Pot Bayou — bayou waterfront, serene, $700K–$2.5M Crescent Lake — central, value, $350K–$600K Old Northeast Most Walkable Historic Homes Median home price: $700K–$1M Old Northeast is St. Pete's most beloved historic neighborhood — a 1.3-square-mile pocket of Craftsman bungalows, Mediterranean Revival homes, and Tudor cottages on brick-paved streets shaded by mature oak canopies. It sits directly north of downtown, bordered by Coffee Pot Bayou to the north and Tampa Bay to the east. What makes Old Northeast stand out is walkability. Residents walk to downtown's waterfront parks, the Saturday Morning Market, Publix, and dozens of restaurants without getting in a car. For relocation buyers coming from Chicago or New York who don't want to give up that pedestrian lifestyle, this is consistently the first neighborhood I show. Homes here range from 1920s bungalows in the $700Ks to fully renovated waterfront estates above $2M. The neighborhood has strong owner-occupancy — this isn't an investor-heavy area — which keeps the streetscape intact and property values stable. Historic designation on many parcels means renovations are reviewed, which protects the character of the block. Price range $700K–$2M+ (waterfront) Property types Craftsman bungalows, Mediterranean Revival, Tudor, waterfront estates Best for Families, professionals, relocation buyers from walkable Northern cities Walk score High — downtown, parks, and dining all within 10 min on foot Schools North Shore Elementary, John Hopkins Middle Watch out for Historic deed restrictions on renovations; flood zone AE in eastern sections Snell Isle Luxury Waterfront Median home price: $1.5M–$3M+ Snell Isle is a private peninsula jutting into Tampa Bay — St. Petersburg's most prestigious address. Developed in the 1920s by C. Perry Snell as a planned residential community, it remains one of the few neighborhoods in the city where nearly every block has water views or direct bay access. Deep-water dockage is the defining feature here. Homes on Brightwaters Boulevard and Snell Isle Boulevard sit on oversized lots with seawalls, boat lifts, and dockage capable of handling vessels 40 feet and larger. For serious boaters, this is the entry point for the Gulf-to-Bay boating corridor — you're in Tampa Bay in minutes and through the Gulf passes in under an hour. Properties range from original 1940s-era Mediterranean estates undergoing renovation to newer construction custom homes pushing $5M+. The neighborhood is quiet, owner-occupied, and has very low turnover — well-priced listings here move fast. I typically advise serious buyers to get pre-approved and be ready to act within 48 hours of a property coming to market. Price range $1M–$5M+ Property types Waterfront estates, deep-water dockage, Mediterranean Revival, new construction custom Best for Luxury relocation buyers, serious boaters, high-net-worth buyers seeking privacy Boating access Direct Tampa Bay access; Gulf passes within 30–45 minutes Watch out for Flood zone AE throughout; flood insurance required, elevation certificates critical Shore Acres Canal Front Boating Median home price: $600K–$1.5M Shore Acres is the neighborhood buyers discover when they fall in love with the waterfront lifestyle but can't justify Snell Isle pricing. It's a grid of canal-front homes on the northeast side of St. Pete with direct boating access to Tampa Bay — typically at $400K–$700K less than comparable Snell Isle properties. The trade-off is lot size and prestige. Shore Acres lots are smaller, the streets are less iconic, and the homes are generally mid-century ranch-style rather than grand estates. But the water access is real — canals connect directly to Tampa Bay, dockage is common, and the boating lifestyle is fully intact. It's also a genuine neighborhood in the traditional sense: kids on bikes, block parties, the kind of community feel that's harder to find in more exclusive enclaves. For families relocating from the Midwest who want waterfront living without paying a Snell Isle premium, Shore Acres consistently delivers. Price range $600K–$1.5M (canal front) Property types Mid-century ranch, updated canal homes, some new construction Best for Families, boating enthusiasts, buyers who want waterfront access at a relative value Boating access Canal to Tampa Bay; smaller vessels recommended for canal navigation Watch out for Flood zone AE; several streets experienced flooding during major storms Downtown St. Petersburg Urban Arts & Culture Condos from $450K; townhomes $700K–$1.4M Downtown St. Pete has undergone one of the most significant transformations of any mid-size American city in the past decade. What was a sleepy waterfront with a few museums is now a genuinely vibrant urban core — walkable, dense with independent restaurants, and anchored by a 2.5-mile waterfront park along Tampa Bay. The Dali Museum, the Mahaffey Theater, the St. Pete Pier, and the Central Avenue corridor (rooftop bars, craft cocktail spots, gallery walks) are all within walking distance of most downtown residences. The Saturday Morning Market runs October through May and draws thousands of residents weekly. Housing is predominantly condos and lofts — the stock that draws empty nesters downsizing from suburban homes, young professionals priced out of Tampa, and investors targeting short-term rental income. High-rise units with water views push $700K–$1.5M. Entry-level condos start around $450K. I advise buyers to carefully review HOA financials and special assessment history before making offers in older downtown buildings — deferred maintenance is a real issue in several complexes. Price range $450K (entry condo) to $1.5M+ (penthouse, townhome) Property types High-rise condos, lofts, townhomes, historic rowhouses Best for Young professionals, empty nesters, investors, urban lifestyle buyers Walk score Very high — most errands, dining, and entertainment walkable Watch out for HOA special assessments in older buildings; parking constraints; flood zone X–AE depending on block Historic Kenwood Arts District First-Time Buyers Median home price: $350K–$550K Historic Kenwood sits just west of downtown and has become one of the most talked-about neighborhoods in St. Pete — equal parts arts district, bungalow preservation zone, and up-and-coming investment corridor. The housing stock is almost entirely Craftsman bungalows from the 1920s and 1930s, many of them meticulously restored by owner-occupants who bought in before prices ran up. The neighborhood has a distinct identity: mural-covered fences, front-yard vegetable gardens, neighbors who actually talk to each other, and a strong LGBTQ+ community presence that has shaped the area's culture for decades. Grand Central District — the commercial corridor running through Kenwood — has independent coffee shops, art galleries, vinyl record stores, and some of the best casual dining in the city. For first-time buyers, Kenwood is one of the last pockets of St. Pete where buying under $400K is still possible and the neighborhood is genuinely on the rise. Values have appreciated significantly since 2019 and that trajectory appears intact. Investors have also taken notice — the flip activity in Kenwood has increased, which means buyers need to scrutinize renovation quality carefully. Price range $320K–$600K Property types Craftsman bungalows, mostly 2BR/1BA to 3BR/2BA Best for First-time buyers, creative professionals, investors, LGBTQ+ community Vibe Artsy, community-driven, independent, walkable to Grand Central Watch out for Flip-renovation quality varies widely; inspect carefully; parking can be tight Tierra Verde Barrier Island STR Investment Median home price: $900K–$2.5M Tierra Verde is the barrier island community at the southern tip of Pinellas County, connected to the mainland by a single causeway and bordered by Fort De Soto County Park — consistently ranked one of the best beaches in the United States. It is, by any measure, island living: water on three sides, boat traffic visible from most back yards, and a pace of life that feels entirely disconnected from the mainland. The boating here is exceptional. Deep-water channels run through the community, giving residents direct access to the Gulf of Mexico through Bunces Pass and the Skyway corridor. It's a legitimate offshore fishing and cruising base. I work with several investors here specifically because of the short-term rental demand — Fort De Soto draws visitors year-round, and well-positioned waterfront rentals generate strong occupancy. The STR regulatory environment in Tierra Verde is more permissive than many Pinellas communities, but buyers should verify current city and HOA rules before purchasing for that purpose. Insurance costs here are higher than mainland St. Pete — flood, wind, and homeowners combined can run $25,000–$45,000 annually on waterfront properties. That number needs to be in your underwriting before you make an offer. Price range $800K–$3M+ (waterfront) Property types Single-family waterfront, deep-water dockage, newer construction Best for Luxury buyers, boating lifestyle, STR investors, buyers seeking true island living STR potential Strong — Fort De Soto proximity drives year-round demand Watch out for Insurance costs $25K–$45K/yr on waterfront; single-causeway access; verify STR zoning Coffee Pot Bayou Bayou Waterfront Luxury Median home price: $800K–$2.5M Coffee Pot Bayou is a narrow tidal bayou that cuts through the north side of St. Pete between Old Northeast and Shore Acres — and the neighborhood that wraps around it is one of the city's best-kept secrets. The streets along the bayou (Coffee Pot Boulevard NE, in particular) offer some of the most scenic waterfront in the city: still water, mangroves, manatees, and paddleboarders, with the downtown skyline visible to the south. Homes here span a wider range than Snell Isle — from fully renovated 1950s ranches in the $800Ks to new construction contemporary homes approaching $3M. The bayou is navigable by kayak, paddleboard, and small skiff, though it doesn't offer the deep-water dockage of Snell Isle or Shore Acres. Buyers who want a serene waterfront setting without the full boating infrastructure tend to find Coffee Pot Bayou a compelling alternative. The neighborhood benefits from Old Northeast's walkability without being technically part of it — you're still a 15-minute walk from downtown and the Saturday market. Inventory is low and turnover is rare, which means when a well-priced property appears here, it attracts serious attention quickly. Price range $700K–$3M Property types Bayou-front single-family, mix of mid-century and new construction Best for Waterfront buyers who want serenity over deep-water dockage; luxury relocation Water access Kayak/paddleboard/small skiff; not suitable for large vessels Watch out for Flood zone AE along the bayou; very low inventory — move quickly when listings appear Crescent Lake Central Location Best Value Median home price: $380K–$600K Crescent Lake sits at the geographic center of St. Pete, surrounding a 22-acre freshwater lake and park that serves as the neighborhood's anchor. It's not a waterfront neighborhood in the Gulf Coast sense — no boat access, no bay views — but it offers something different: a quiet, established residential feel with easy access to everything. From Crescent Lake, downtown is a 10-minute drive, I-275 is 5 minutes away, and the beaches are 20 minutes. The housing stock is a mix of original 1940s–60s bungalows and ranch homes alongside newer infill construction, with prices that remain accessible relative to the rest of central St. Pete. For professionals relocating to St. Pete who need to commute occasionally to Tampa, Crescent Lake often makes sense that neighborhoods further north don't. It's also a strong value play for buyers who want central St. Pete access without the premium of Old Northeast or Downtown. I've placed several Chicago transplants here who were pleasantly surprised by what their budget could buy.
