Florida Hometown Heroes Program 2026: Who Qualifies, How Much You Get, and How to Apply in Pinellas County

What is the Florida Hometown Heroes Program, and how much can you get in 2026?

Florida's Hometown Heroes Program provides up to $35,000 in down payment and closing cost assistance — structured as a 0% interest deferred second mortgage — to eligible homebuyers who work full-time for qualifying employers in healthcare, education, law enforcement, fire, military, and other essential services. In Pinellas County, income limits for 2026 are $172,050 for TBA loan borrowers, or $114,700–$131,905 for household income under the Bond program. The 2026 funding round launched July 13 with $50 million available on a first-come, first-served basis.

By Amanda LeGault, Licensed FL REALTOR® (#SL3506757) | September 21, 2026

If you're buying a home in St. Petersburg and you work in healthcare, education, law enforcement, or the military — or for any number of other essential employers — there's a program that can put up to $35,000 toward your down payment and closing costs. And most of the people who qualify for it have no idea they do.

Florida's Hometown Heroes Program is one of the best-funded down payment assistance programs in the state, and in Pinellas County it's genuinely usable — the income limits are high enough to cover a lot of dual-income households, and the loan caps work for most of the St. Pete market. Here's what you need to know.

Who qualifies — and it's broader than you think

The program's eligibility is based on where you work, not your specific job title. That distinction matters. If you work full-time for any of the following types of employers, you qualify:

  • Healthcare facilities (hospitals, clinics, assisted living, home health agencies)
  • K-12 schools (public or private)
  • Fire departments and ambulance services
  • Law enforcement and correctional agencies
  • Courts
  • Licensed childcare facilities
  • Military (active duty or veteran)

The "any full-time employee" rule is what surprises people. A cafeteria worker at a hospital qualifies. An administrative coordinator at a school qualifies. A custodian at a fire station qualifies. It's about the employer, not the role.

You also have to be a first-time homebuyer — which Florida defines as someone who hasn't owned a primary residence in the past three years. And you need to occupy the home as your primary residence.

How much assistance can you get?

The Hometown Heroes Program offers 5% of your first mortgage loan amount, with a minimum of $10,000 and a maximum of $35,000. Loans of $200,000 or less receive the full $10,000 minimum regardless of the 5% calculation.

On a $600,000 purchase with 5% down and a $570,000 first mortgage, for example, the assistance would be $28,500. On a $700,000 purchase with 5% down, you'd hit close to the $35,000 cap.

Important: This is a deferred loan, not a grant. Nothing is forgiven, but nothing is owed while you live in the home as your primary residence. Repayment is triggered when you sell, refinance, transfer the deed, pay off your first mortgage, or move out. At that point, you repay the original amount — no interest, no appreciation sharing, just the dollar amount you received.

Pinellas County income and loan limits for 2026

The 2026 income limits for Pinellas County (effective May 6, 2026):

TBA loan program (borrower income only): Income limit: $172,050

Bond loan program (total household income — all occupants 18+ and any spouse):

  • 1–2 person household: $114,700
  • 3+ person household: $131,905

The TBA limits use only the credit-qualifying borrowers on the loan. The Bond limits count total household income, including anyone living in the home — so two working adults, even if only one is on the loan, both count under Bond.

The TBA conventional cap gives you meaningful room in the St. Pete market. For buyers in the $500K–$700K range — which is a significant chunk of the Pinellas inventory — the program can apply, depending on the loan structure.

The 2026 funding situation

The 2026 round launched July 13 with $50 million in available funding. Hometown Heroes runs first-come, first-served — when the money runs out, it runs out until the state allocates more. That said, Florida has refunded the program multiple times since it launched in 2022, and it has historically remained available through most of the year.

The practical takeaway: don't wait until you've found your house to apply. Get pre-approved first so your lender can reserve the funds as soon as you're under contract.

What loan types work with the program

  • FHA loans — most common for buyers with smaller down payments or credit scores in the 620–680 range
  • VA loans — for eligible veterans and active-duty military; can be combined with the DPA for closing cost coverage
  • USDA loans — for eligible rural areas (limited in most of urban Pinellas)
  • Conventional loans — for buyers with stronger credit (680+); the higher loan cap makes this attractive in St. Pete's price range

Your lender handles all of this. You apply through a Florida Housing-approved lender, not directly through the state.

How to apply: the five-step process

  1. Verify your employer qualifies — your lender will confirm this, but you can also check Florida Housing's employer list. If your employer is a hospital, school system, government agency, or licensed facility in the categories above, you almost certainly qualify.
  2. Get pre-approved — this takes 1–3 business days with an approved lender. The lender will check your income against Pinellas County limits and confirm which loan program you qualify for.
  3. Find your home — once pre-approved, you're shopping with a real budget. The DPA is reserved when you go under contract, not before.
  4. Complete homebuyer education — a short online course is required. Takes about 4–6 hours and costs around $99.
  5. Close — the DPA funds are applied at closing. You're not writing a second check for the down payment; it's handled in the closing documents.

Total timeline from pre-approval to closing: 30–45 days in most cases.

The question I get most often

"If I sell in five years, how much do I owe back?"

Exactly what you received — the principal amount, nothing more. So if you got $28,500 in DPA, you repay $28,500 at sale or refinance. No interest. No appreciation sharing. The program is designed to get you into a home, not to participate in your equity upside.

If you're considering a condo in downtown St. Pete or a single-family home in Shore Acres with this program, it's worth running the numbers with a lender before you dismiss it — especially with the conventional loan cap at $832,750 in Pinellas. The program has more reach in this market than most people realize.

Frequently Asked Questions

Who qualifies for the Florida Hometown Heroes Program in 2026?

Any full-time employee of a qualifying employer qualifies — including healthcare facilities, K-12 schools, fire departments, law enforcement agencies, courts, licensed childcare facilities, and military. Eligibility is based on where you work, not your specific job title. You must also be a first-time homebuyer (no primary residence ownership in the past three years) and plan to occupy the purchased home as your primary residence.

How much down payment assistance does Hometown Heroes provide?

The program provides 5% of your first mortgage loan amount, with a minimum of $10,000 and a maximum of $35,000. On a $600,000 purchase with a $570,000 first mortgage, that's $28,500. The assistance is structured as a 0% interest deferred second mortgage — no monthly payments, repaid only when you sell, refinance, or move out.

What are the income limits for Hometown Heroes in Pinellas County in 2026?

For the TBA loan program, the borrower income limit is $172,050 (counting only borrowers on the loan). For the Bond program, the household income limit is $114,700 for 1–2 person households and $131,905 for 3+ person households (counting all occupants 18 and older, plus any spouse). These limits were effective May 6, 2026 — verify with your lender before applying.

Is the Hometown Heroes grant forgiven?

No — it's a deferred loan, not a grant. The original amount is repaid when you sell, refinance, transfer the deed, pay off your first mortgage, or stop using the home as your primary residence. No interest accrues. Nothing is forgiven.

Can I use Hometown Heroes to buy a condo in St. Petersburg?

Yes, condos are eligible — but the condo project must meet the approval requirements of your chosen loan type (FHA, VA, or conventional). Condominiums using FHA financing require FHA condo project approval, and conventionally financed condos need to meet Fannie/Freddie project eligibility standards. Your lender will confirm whether a specific building qualifies.

Florida's Hometown Heroes Program is one of the most genuinely useful down payment tools in the state — and in Pinellas County, the income caps are high enough that it works for a lot of buyers in the $500K–$700K range. The catch is funding availability, so timing matters.
