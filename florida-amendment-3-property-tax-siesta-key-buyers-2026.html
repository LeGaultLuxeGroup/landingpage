Florida Amendment 3 and Your Siesta Key Property Taxes: The Non-Homestead Buyer's Guide to the 5% Cap (2026–2027)

How does Florida Amendment 3 affect Siesta Key buyers who won't be claiming homestead?

Most Siesta Key buyers are purchasing a second home, vacation rental, or seasonal property—which means they won't qualify for the expanded homestead exemption under Amendment 3 ($150,000 in 2027, $250,000 in 2028, on non-school taxes only). But the amendment still benefits them: the annual assessed-value cap on non-homestead property dropped from 10% to 5% on non-school taxes, effective with the 2027 tax year. For a $1.5M–$2M Siesta Key condo, that change limits how fast your tax bill can grow year over year—a meaningful difference over a 5–10 year hold in a rising market.

What Amendment 3 Did—and Didn't—Change for Non-Homestead Owners

Amendment 3 made two major changes to Florida's property tax structure. Only one of them applies to second-home and vacation rental buyers.

The change that doesn't apply to you: The expanded homestead exemption. The exemption on non-school property taxes rises from the current ~$51,000 to $150,000 in 2027 and $250,000 in 2028. This applies only to homesteaded primary residences. If Siesta Key isn't your permanent, year-round home—and you haven't declared it as your Florida homestead—you don't get this exemption. No action you take before December 31 changes that if you're not relocating here as a primary resident.

The change that does apply to you: The non-homestead assessment cap. Before Amendment 3, Florida allowed the assessed value on non-homestead properties to increase by up to 10% per year on non-school taxes. That cap was established by a 2008 constitutional amendment. Amendment 3 cuts it in half—to 5% per year—starting with the 2027 tax year. This applies automatically to every non-homestead property in Florida. No filing, no deadline, no action required on your part.

Important clarification on school taxes: Both Amendment 3 changes—the expanded homestead exemption and the new 5% non-homestead cap—apply to non-school taxes only. School taxes were not changed by Amendment 3. The existing $25,000 school tax exemption for homesteaded properties remains in place, and non-homestead properties are still subject to school taxes without a cap override.

What the 5% Cap Means for Your Siesta Key Tax Bill

Property taxes in Sarasota County have two components: the assessed value of your property (set by the Sarasota County Property Appraiser each January) and the millage rate (set annually by the county, city, school board, and special districts). Amendment 3 affects only the first component—the assessed value—not the millage rate.

Under the old 10% cap, a Siesta Key property's assessed value could have climbed aggressively in a rising market. Under the new 5% cap, that growth is significantly constrained.

Here's an example. You close on a $1.8M Siesta Key beachfront condo in December 2026. The Sarasota County Property Appraiser assesses your property near the purchase price in 2027. Combined millage in Siesta Key (Sarasota County + City of Sarasota + school + special districts) runs approximately 14–16 mills depending on the specific location.

These are illustrative figures—the actual millage rate changes annually, and your assessed value may not hit the cap in lower-appreciation years. But the direction is clear.

If You're Buying Siesta Key as a Vacation Rental

Short-term rental (STR) owners on Siesta Key are non-homestead by definition. The property is not your primary residence, so there's no homestead exemption available to you. The 5% cap applies automatically starting in 2027 and limits assessed-value growth just as it does for second-home buyers.

What you also need to budget for is Sarasota County's tourist development tax—commonly called the bed tax. That's a 6% tax on gross short-term rental revenue, collected through the booking platform (Airbnb, VRBO) or directly by you if you manage bookings yourself. This tax is separate from your annual property taxes and is entirely unaffected by Amendment 3.

What About the December 31 Deadline?

You've probably seen a lot of urgency around December 31 in connection with Amendment 3. Here's who that deadline actually affects:

Primary residence buyers relocating to Siesta Key: To qualify for the expanded homestead exemption starting in 2027 ($150,000 on non-school taxes), you must establish Florida residency and meet the homestead requirements by December 31, 2026. Miss that date, and you'll wait until 2028 to access the first tier—and 2029 for the $250,000 tier. If you're moving here full-time, that timeline matters. You can read the full breakdown in the Sarasota County Amendment 3 guide.

Second-home and vacation rental buyers: The December 31 deadline does not apply to you in any meaningful way relative to Amendment 3. The 5% non-homestead cap takes effect in 2027 for all non-homestead properties automatically. You don't need to do anything before December 31 specifically because of Amendment 3.

What you should be thinking about before year-end: reviewing your property taxes as part of your total carrying cost analysis before you close. If you're under contract right now, your contract likely includes property tax prorations based on the current assessed value—which may be significantly lower than what the Property Appraiser will assess after a sale at today's market price. That's a carrying cost conversation worth having before you finalize your numbers.

The Bigger Picture for Siesta Key Buyers

The 5% cap is a meaningful improvement in predictability for non-homestead owners—and predictability matters when you're buying in a market where values have held at $1M+ and there's no sign of that changing.

It won't eliminate your tax bill, and it doesn't protect you from millage rate increases. But it limits one of the two main drivers of property tax growth, and in an appreciating coastal market like Siesta Key, that protection compounds meaningfully over time.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does Florida Amendment 3 benefit Siesta Key second-home buyers who can't claim homestead?

Yes. Even though second-home and vacation rental buyers on Siesta Key won't qualify for the new expanded homestead exemption ($150K in 2027, $250K in 2028, on non-school taxes), they still benefit from the other half of Amendment 3: the annual assessed-value cap on non-homestead properties dropped from 10% to 5% on non-school taxes starting with the 2027 tax year. That cap applies automatically to all non-homestead property—no filing required.

What is the non-homestead assessment cap under Florida Amendment 3?

Before Amendment 3, Florida capped annual assessed-value increases on non-homestead properties (second homes, vacation rentals, investment properties) at 10% per year on non-school taxes. Amendment 3, passed November 2026, cuts that cap to 5% per year beginning in 2027. The cap applies to county, city, and most special district taxes—school taxes were not changed by Amendment 3.

Can I claim Florida homestead exemption on a Siesta Key vacation home?

No. Florida's homestead exemption applies only to your permanent primary residence—the place you live and intend to remain. A Siesta Key property used as a second home, vacation rental, or seasonal home does not qualify, regardless of how often you stay there. If you're relocating to Siesta Key full-time as your primary residence, you can file for homestead with the Sarasota County Property Appraiser by March 1 of the year following your move.

What is Sarasota County tourist development tax and does Amendment 3 affect it?

Sarasota County charges a 6% tourist development tax (also called the bed tax) on gross short-term rental revenue, collected either through the booking platform or directly by the owner. This is separate from your annual ad valorem property taxes and is not affected by Amendment 3. If you're buying Siesta Key as a vacation rental investment, budget for this tax on top of your property tax bill.

How much can a Siesta Key second home's assessed value increase each year under Amendment 3?

Under Amendment 3, the assessed value on your Siesta Key second home or vacation rental can increase by no more than 5% per year on non-school taxes, down from 10% previously. The actual millage rate is set annually by Sarasota County, the City, and other taxing authorities, so your bill can still rise if the rate increases—but the assessed-value cap limits one of the two main drivers of property tax growth.
