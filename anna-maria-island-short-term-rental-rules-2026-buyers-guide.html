Anna Maria Island Short-Term Rental Rules (2026): What Every Buyer Must Know Before Closing

What are the short-term rental rules on Anna Maria Island in 2026?

Short-term rental rules on Anna Maria Island are not island-wide — they vary by city. Anna Maria City requires a Florida DBPR transient lodging license and caps occupancy at 8 persons for certain properties. Holmes Beach has the strictest regulations, with minimum stays ranging from 7 to 30 days depending on the zoning district. Bradenton Beach requires owner registration and enforces occupancy limits using specific city formulas. Most importantly: HOA and condo association rules can override every city ordinance — potentially prohibiting short-term rentals or imposing 30–90 day minimum stays regardless of what the city allows. Always verify both the city code and the HOA documents before you close.

Here's the conversation I have at least once a week with buyers looking at Anna Maria Island property: "Can I rent this out short-term?" The answer is almost always yes — but what that actually means, and whether the investment pencils the way they think it will, depends entirely on which city the property sits in, which zoning district within that city, and what the HOA documents say.

Buyers consistently assume Anna Maria Island operates under one uniform set of rules. It doesn't. The island spans three separate municipalities — the City of Anna Maria, Holmes Beach, and Bradenton Beach — and each one has written its own vacation rental playbook. Get this wrong, and a $1.5 million purchase that was supposed to generate $150,000 a year in rental income turns into a property you can only rent to long-term tenants or use yourself.

This guide maps out what each city actually requires in 2026, explains when HOA rules override everything, walks through the licensing you'll need, and tells you exactly what to check before you write an offer.

The Three-City Breakdown

When you're looking at Anna Maria Island real estate, the first question isn't "can I rent it?" — it's "which city is this property in?"

City of Anna Maria

The northernmost city on the island operates a registration-based system. To rent your property short-term, you'll need to register with the City of Anna Maria, obtain a Florida DBPR transient public lodging license (more on that below), open a Manatee County tax collector account, and complete state tax registration for tourist development tax collection.

Annual inspections are required — you request one by email, and the city schedules the walk-through. Occupancy is capped at eight persons for many properties, though the exact limit depends on the property's square footage and configuration.

In terms of short-term rental flexibility, Anna Maria City sits in the middle of the island's three municipalities. It's not the most permissive, but it's more workable than Holmes Beach for investors who need short-stay bookings to drive returns.

Holmes Beach

This is where most buyers get tripped up — and where the stakes are highest, because Holmes Beach has some of the most desirable condos and single-family homes on the island.

Holmes Beach requires a vacation rental certificate before you can rent at all. But the more significant issue is the minimum stay requirement, which is determined by your zoning district. Depending on where your property sits within Holmes Beach, you may be looking at a 7-day minimum, a 14-day minimum, or a 30-day minimum. That's not a typo. A 30-day minimum effectively converts a vacation rental into a monthly rental — which carries dramatically different income potential than a property that can be booked week-by-week during peak season.

Before you fall in love with a Holmes Beach property, find out which zoning district it's in and what the minimum stay requirement is. This information lives in the city's vacation rental ordinance and the property's zoning designation — your agent should be able to pull this for you before you get emotionally invested.

Bradenton Beach

The southernmost city on the island has its own approach. Bradenton Beach requires owner registration with supporting documentation and enforces occupancy using formulas specific to property type and size. Under certain conditions, properties can accommodate up to twelve occupants — which makes Bradenton Beach attractive for larger groups and can support higher nightly rates on bigger homes.

The registration process is more documentation-intensive than Anna Maria City, but the rules are generally considered more investor-friendly than Holmes Beach for properties that qualify.

The Rule That Can Override Everything: Your HOA

Here's what every single buyer must understand before purchasing vacation rental property on Anna Maria Island: municipal ordinances set the floor, not the ceiling. Your HOA or condo association can be significantly more restrictive — and their rules will govern your property regardless of what the city allows.

A condo in Holmes Beach might sit in a 7-day-minimum zone, and you might be planning on weekly rentals. But if the condo association's CC&Rs prohibit rentals shorter than 30 days — or shorter than 90 days, or at all — the city's 7-day permission is irrelevant. The condo docs win.

I've seen this situation play out badly for buyers who didn't do the homework. They purchase what looks like an ideal vacation rental, close, try to list on Airbnb, and then discover the association prohibits it. There's no appeal. There's no workaround. The HOA has the legal authority to enforce its rental restrictions, and the city can't override it.

Before you make an offer on any Anna Maria Island property you're planning to rent short-term, request the full HOA or condo association documents — specifically the Declaration of Covenants, Conditions, and Restrictions (CC&Rs), the bylaws, and any rental restriction addenda. Review them carefully, or have an attorney review them. This is not optional.

The DBPR License and County Tax Registration

Regardless of which city your property is in, operating a short-term rental in Florida requires a Florida Department of Business and Professional Regulation transient public lodging license. This is a state-level requirement, separate from whatever the city requires locally.

You'll also need to register with the Manatee County tax collector for tourist development tax collection. This is the county-level vacation rental tax (often called the "bed tax"), and you're responsible for collecting it from guests and remitting it to the county. Failure to collect and remit puts you personally on the hook for the unpaid taxes.

The full licensing stack for an Anna Maria Island short-term rental looks something like this:

  • Florida DBPR transient public lodging license — state-level, required everywhere
  • Florida Department of Revenue sales tax registration — for collecting and remitting state sales tax on rental income
  • Manatee County tax collector account — for tourist development tax
  • City-specific registration or certificate — varies by Anna Maria City, Holmes Beach, or Bradenton Beach
  • Annual inspection — required by Anna Maria City; Holmes Beach and Bradenton Beach have their own compliance requirements

Platforms like Airbnb and Vrbo can handle the tax collection and remittance in many Florida markets, but you're still responsible for confirming everything is properly set up. The licensing is your responsibility as the property owner.

The Real-World Example: Why This Matters at a Transaction Level

Let me give you a concrete comparison that illustrates why city and zoning matter so much.

Consider two properties: a two-bedroom condo in Holmes Beach priced at $1.1 million, and a three-bedroom single-family home in Anna Maria City priced at $1.4 million. On the surface, the condo looks like the better investment — lower acquisition cost, easier to manage, strong demand for condos on AMI.

But if the Holmes Beach condo sits in a 30-day minimum zone, the income profile changes completely. You can't capture the $6,000–$8,000 peak-week bookings that drive vacation rental returns. You're competing with monthly rentals, not vacationers — different demand, different rates, different seasonal patterns.

The Anna Maria City home, meanwhile, may have an HOA that imposes a 30-night minimum stay on all rentals — not because the city requires it, but because the community voted it in. Same result, different mechanism.

Your specific numbers depend on the property's location within the city, its zoning district, the HOA documents, and what rental history looks like. That's not something you can determine from a listing — it requires pulling the actual ordinance, confirming the zoning, and reviewing the governing documents. This is exactly where working with a local agent who knows the island pays off.

What to Verify Before You Write an Offer

When you're evaluating any Anna Maria Island property as a vacation rental investment, run through this checklist before you get emotionally invested in a purchase:

  1. Confirm which city the property is in — don't assume. The island spans three municipalities, and boundaries aren't always obvious from a listing.
  2. Pull the city's vacation rental ordinance — confirm whether the property requires registration or a certificate, and what the minimum stay requirement is for that zoning district.
  3. Request the full HOA/condo documents — specifically the CC&Rs and any rental restriction addenda. Review before making an offer, not after.
  4. Verify the DBPR and county licensing requirements — confirm what's already in place (if the property has been rented before) or what you'll need to set up.
  5. Request rental history — if the seller has been operating the property as a short-term rental, ask for actual booking history and income statements. Projected income is marketing; historical income is evidence.
  6. Confirm existing bookings — if there are active reservations, understand how those will transfer (or not) at closing.

Frequently Asked Questions

Are short-term rentals allowed on Anna Maria Island?

Yes, short-term rentals are allowed on Anna Maria Island, but the rules vary significantly by city. Anna Maria City, Holmes Beach, and Bradenton Beach each have their own registration requirements, minimum stay periods, and occupancy caps. Beyond city rules, HOA and condo association restrictions can override municipal ordinances entirely — potentially prohibiting short-term rentals or imposing minimum stays of 30–90 days regardless of what the city allows. Always verify both the city code and the HOA documents before closing.

Which city on Anna Maria Island has the strictest vacation rental rules?

Holmes Beach has the most restrictive vacation rental regulations on Anna Maria Island. Minimum stay requirements range from 7 to 30 days depending on the zoning district, and owners must obtain a vacation rental certificate before operating. A property in a 30-day-minimum zone can't capture the weekly vacation bookings that drive peak-season rental income — which significantly affects the investment's return profile compared to what the purchase price might suggest.

What licenses do I need to rent my Anna Maria Island property short-term?

At minimum, you'll need a Florida DBPR transient public lodging license, a Manatee County tax collector account for tourist development tax collection, and Florida state tax registration. Each city on the island also requires its own local registration or certificate — Anna Maria City requires registration and an annual inspection, Holmes Beach requires a vacation rental certificate, and Bradenton Beach requires owner registration with supporting documentation. All of these are your responsibility as the property owner, even if you use a property management company.

Can an HOA prevent me from renting my Anna Maria Island property short-term?

Yes — and this is the most critical thing to verify before closing. HOA and condo association rules can completely override what the city allows. Even if Holmes Beach permits 7-day minimums in your zone, a condo association can impose a 30- or 90-day minimum, or prohibit short-term rentals entirely. Always request and review the full HOA documents — specifically the Declaration of Covenants, Conditions, and Restrictions (CC&Rs) — before making an offer on any vacation rental investment property on Anna Maria Island.

How much do Anna Maria Island vacation rental properties typically earn?

Rental income on Anna Maria Island varies widely based on property type, city, zoning district, minimum stay rules, and HOA restrictions. A single-family home in Anna Maria City with no HOA and no minimum stay restriction beyond DBPR licensing requirements will generate significantly more income than a Holmes Beach condo in a 30-day-minimum zone. Before purchasing, request actual rental history from the seller if the property has operated as a rental — projected income numbers are a starting point, not a guarantee.
